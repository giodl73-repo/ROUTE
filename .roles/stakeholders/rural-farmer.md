# Rural Farmer

## Who this is

A row crop, livestock, or specialty crop producer who depends on moving product to a grain elevator, livestock market, processing facility, or export terminal. May be operating semi trucks on farm, or relying on local carriers. The highway system's weight limits, clearances, and distance from farm to on-ramp directly affect profitability.

## Primary concerns

**Distance to on-ramp**: Every mile of county road between the farm and an interstate on-ramp is a mile at lower speed, higher cost, and higher wear on equipment. For grain harvest, when the elevator is 80 miles away and the harvest window is 2 weeks, distance matters enormously.

**Weight limits**: Standard 80,000 lb GVWR may not be sufficient for full grain loads. States that allow higher weights on designated routes create economic advantage for farmers in those states. Older bridges with lower posted limits create detour requirements during harvest.

**Harvest window logistics**: Grain harvest happens in a narrow window. A corridor that's chronically congested during September and October is a direct cost — grain prices move while trucks sit in traffic.

**Export access**: A farmer in Iowa ultimately depends on the efficiency of the Mississippi River barge system or Gulf export terminals. The highway system that connects farms to elevators and elevators to terminals is the agricultural supply chain's physical infrastructure.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Average distance from agricultural land to nearest interstate on-ramp (county-level analysis)
- Weight limit exceptions and oversize/overweight permit requirements by state on the corridor
- Harvest season congestion patterns on agricultural freight corridors
- Proximity of corridor to major grain export terminals or livestock processing centers

## Lens for scoring

The rural farmer grounds C2 (Rural Connectivity) in specific agricultural supply chain reality. A corridor that passes through agricultural land but has no on-ramps within 40 miles of major farming areas, or has weight restrictions that prohibit full grain loads, is not providing the connectivity C2 is meant to measure.
