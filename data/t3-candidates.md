# Interstate 2.0 — T3 Candidate List

**Date**: 2026-05-07  
**Rubric**: v1.2

## T3 Positive Definition

T3 is not "whatever didn't make T2." T3 has a specific, affirmative purpose: **connecting communities to the interstate system that would otherwise be disconnected.**

A corridor earns T3 designation by meeting at least one of five coverage criteria:

| Criterion | Threshold | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Serves counties currently >30 miles from any T1/T2 on-ramp | Closes the 30-mile access gap |
| **Agricultural access** | Primary route from zone with >$500M annual production to T1/T2, within 25 miles | Farm-to-market freight access |
| **Evacuation backbone** | Designated state emergency management primary route with no T1/T2 alternative | Life-safety |
| **Healthcare corridor** | Removes a rural county from >45-min trauma transport time | Life-safety |
| **State/major city gateway** | Connects a population center >100k to T1/T2 where none exists | Economic access |

**T3 is not T4 if it meets any criterion above.** A 50-mile T3 spur serving 20,000 isolated rural residents may have a higher NPV per dollar than a T2 capacity expansion.

**US highways upgrade to T3 when:** the corridor meets any criterion AND full interstate upgrade (grade separation, full access control, all-weather) is technically feasible. The upgrade is warranted even at low current AADT if the access need is real.

## Proposed T3 — US Highway Upgrades

The most important T3 upgrade candidates serve genuine coverage gaps confirmed by our data.

### Priority 1: Maine Northern Corridor

**Coverage gap**: The worst coverage gap east of the Mississippi. Aroostook County (261mi), Washington County (202mi), Piscataquis (202mi), Penobscot (184mi), Somerset (159mi), Hancock (156mi). 14 Maine counties containing 700,000+ people are all >90 miles from any interstate.

**Corridor**: US-2 / US-1 alignment from Augusta ME northward to Houlton ME (Canada border), with east-west connector through Bangor area.  
**Miles**: ~300 miles of new/upgraded alignment  
**Proposed designation**: State-specific I-9X or spur designation  
**Function**: Coverage (14 Maine gap counties), Healthcare (rural Maine hospitals closing rapidly), Agricultural (potato, timber, blueberry export), Evacuation (coastal Maine has no evacuation route inland)  
**Cost**: ~$9B at $30M/mi upgrade rate  
**Priority**: Highest in eastern US — worst absolute coverage gap

### Priority 2: US-12 (Northern Plains coverage corridor)

**Score**: 14.0/150, B1=10.0, 620 miles in TIGER  
**Full alignment**: Aberdeen SD → Rapid City SD → Billings MT (~600 miles)  
**Function**: Coverage (serves South Dakota Black Hills, western SD ranching), Agricultural (beef/wheat export), B1=10.0 confirms maximum isolation between I-90 (south) and I-94 (north)  
**Proposed designation**: T3 spur designation; potentially absorbed into I-90 extension as I-90N

### Priority 3: US-14 (Wyoming/SD northern tier filler)

**Score**: 13.3/150, B1=9.3, 455 miles  
**Full alignment**: Rapid City SD through Wyoming to Yellowstone area  
**Function**: Coverage + tourism (Yellowstone gateway), Agricultural (eastern Wyoming cattle)  
**B1=9.3** confirms high isolation — the I-90/I-25 gap in northern Wyoming is real

### Priority 4: US-191 (Mountain West coverage)

**Score**: 9.8/150, B1=8.4, 227 miles  
**Alignment**: Gallup NM → I-40 north to I-70 via eastern Utah  
**Function**: Coverage (eastern Utah counties with no interstate), Navajo Nation access (healthcare, evacuation), Agricultural  
**Note**: Part of this alignment is critical for Navajo Nation access to healthcare

### Priority 5: US-395 (Pacific Inland T3)

**Score**: 11.1/150, B1=8.1, 361 miles  
**Full alignment**: Reno NV → Spokane WA  
**Function**: Coverage (eastern Oregon desert counties), Agricultural (Yakima Valley approach in WA), connects I-80 to I-90 through interior west without going through Cascades  
**Note**: Complements US-97/I-11 proposal; eastern alignment alternative

### Priority 6: US-93 (Great Basin coverage)

**Score**: 9.6/150, B1=7.9, 293 miles  
**Full alignment**: Las Vegas NV → Twin Falls ID  
**Function**: Coverage (central Nevada counties), connects I-15 to I-84 through the Basin and Range gap  
**Note**: Works with I-11 (US-97) as parallel interior west corridor

## T3 Standards (from tier-standards.md)

- PTI target: ≤ 1.50 (functional reliability; no guarantee)
- EV charging: ≥ 50kW every 100 miles
- Rest areas: 200 miles, 10 truck spaces, basic
- Transit: demand-responsive connection to nearest T2 stop
- Rural access spurs: ≤10 miles, for communities >5k pop >30mi from T1/T2/T3
- Bridge target: fair+ by 2040
- Capacity: no expansion except safety-critical widening
- **Key T3 investment**: the spur program — short connectors to isolated communities is often higher NPV than lengthening a T3 corridor

## The T3 Spur Program

For T3 coverage purposes, the most cost-effective investment is often NOT a full T3 corridor but a **T3 access spur**: a ≤10-mile connector from a community to the nearest existing T3 or T2 corridor.

**Coverage math**: 
- Full T3 interstate (~100 miles): $3B, serves ~20 gap counties along the alignment
- T3 spurs (10 × $10M each = $100M): serves 50-100 isolated communities directly

For rural communities 50-100 miles from the nearest T3 interstate but only 5-10 miles from a US highway with easy interstate-grade alignment — the spur is 30× more cost-effective.

**Priority spur locations** (from coverage-gaps.csv, communities >5k pop, >50mi from any interstate, within 10mi of feasible alignment):
- Unidentified communities in central Nebraska Sand Hills (Logan, McPherson, Grant counties)
- Remote Kentucky Appalachian communities (Metcalfe, Casey counties)
- Mississippi Delta communities (Bolivar, Humphreys counties)
- Eastern Oklahoma rural communities (Latimer, Pushmataha counties)
