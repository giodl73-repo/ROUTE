# Intercity Traveler

## Who this is

A person driving 200+ miles between cities, typically for personal reasons (family, leisure, relocation) or business travel. May be in a conventional vehicle or an EV. Cares about predictability, services, safety, and the experience of the drive — not just the average speed.

## Primary concerns

**Service gaps**: Long stretches of interstate with no fuel, food, or facilities are a quality-of-life and safety issue. The standard for an acceptable service gap has compressed as EV adoption increases — a 200-mile EV range with no fast charger for 180 miles is a genuine stranded-motorist risk.

**EV charging**: The transition to electric vehicles creates a new infrastructure requirement. DC fast chargers at ≤50-mile intervals on major corridors is the threshold for practical EV intercity travel. Many corridors, particularly in rural areas, are below this threshold today.

**Rest area quality**: Federal rest areas on many corridors are aging, understaffed, and inadequate. A rest area that provides a parking space and a pit toilet meets the minimum standard; one that provides safe, well-lit parking, clean facilities, wi-fi, vending, and EV charging is a qualitatively different experience.

**Work zone safety**: Construction zones are the most dangerous part of most highway trips. Work zone design, lighting, and speed enforcement directly affect safety and have gotten worse as maintenance backlogs have grown.

**Weather and information**: Road condition information, weather alerts, and dynamic messaging have improved but remain inconsistent across corridors and states. Travelers making long-distance decisions need reliable real-time information.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Service area spacing (fuel, food, lodging) at regular intervals
- DC fast charger density along the corridor
- Rest area condition ratings (FHWA or state assessments)
- Work zone frequency as a safety and reliability indicator

## Lens for scoring

The intercity traveler grounds A3 (Speed Reliability) and D3 (Infrastructure Vintage) in the actual travel experience. A corridor that is theoretically fast but has chronic work zones, poor rest areas, and no EV infrastructure is underperforming for this stakeholder even if the V/C ratio looks acceptable.
