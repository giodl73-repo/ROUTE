# Relay Hubs: The Aviation Model For Freight

## Research Question

How should ROUTE explain relay hubs as part of Interstate 2.0 without claiming
that labor, EV charging, autonomous freight, safety, utilization, or ROI benefits
are proven?

## Decision This Supports

This report supports the communications decision to make relay hubs the primary
human and operational bridge in the Interstate 2.0 story.

Relay hubs should be used to explain how a service hierarchy becomes an operating
system: drivers, charging, staging, handoffs, maintenance, resilience, and future
automation all need nodes. But relay hubs must remain evidence-gated until
operating assumptions, site feasibility, labor review, safety review, utilization
evidence, and cost/benefit sources close.

## Executive Thesis

Pilots do not fly coast to coast without crew bases, duty rules, handoffs,
maintenance systems, and hub operations. Premium freight should be able to evolve
the same way.

In Interstate 2.0, relay hubs are not decorative stops. They are scheduled
operating points where a national service promise becomes human, fundable, and
testable:

```text
timed freight promise
  -> staffed relay node
  -> charging / staging / maintenance
  -> handoff / marketplace / resilience checkpoint
  -> evidence-backed pilot
```

## Findings

### ROUTE-RELAY-01 — Relay hubs are the best human story in Interstate 2.0.

**Source references:** `README.md` lines 127-143;
`docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 172-192.

**Observed constraint:** The relay concept ties an abstract national service
promise to work people can understand: drivers, shifts, charging, maintenance,
handoffs, staging, state assets, and future automation.

**Implication:** Political and funder materials should use relay hubs to make
Interstate 2.0 feel operational rather than just cartographic.

**Confidence:** High for communications; not proof of labor or operating
benefits.

### ROUTE-RELAY-02 — The aviation analogy is useful because it explains nodes.

**Source references:** `README.md` lines 127-131;
`docs/reports/interstate-2-0-doctrine-report.md` lines 207-211.

**Observed constraint:** The analogy does not claim trucking is aviation. It
borrows the operating pattern: crew bases, duty rules, maintenance, handoffs,
and hubs.

**Implication:** The analogy should be used to explain why freight needs known
operating nodes, not to imply regulatory or economic equivalence with aviation.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### ROUTE-RELAY-03 — Relay hubs turn timed promises into schedulable operations.

**Source references:** `docs/briefs/industry-value-brief.md` lines 74-87;
`docs/reports/route-roi-cost-framework.md` lines 147-160.

**Observed constraint:** Relay hubs can be described as crew bases, charging
depots, transfer points, marketplace nodes, resilience checkpoints, and state
assets, but each function has different evidence needs.

**Implication:** A relay-hub pilot should not be one generic study. It should
name which function it is testing and which evidence would promote or hold the
claim.

**Confidence:** High for structuring pilots; heuristic for outcomes.

### ROUTE-RELAY-04 — Driver benefits are story-ready but not proven.

**Source references:** `README.md` lines 133-140;
`docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 180-192.

**Observed constraint:** The story says professional relay drivers can work
regional shifts and return home. The research conclusions label freight
chauffeurs as story-ready concept and warn not to claim labor or safety benefits
as proven.

**Implication:** Use "path toward safer, more predictable regional work" rather
than "solves driver shortage" or "improves safety."

**Confidence:** Medium.

### ROUTE-RELAY-05 — EV and AV claims must stay staged.

**Source references:** `README.md` lines 137-140;
`docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 180-192;
`docs/briefs/funder-value-brief.md` lines 55-64.

**Observed constraint:** Current materials allow EV support and future AV trunk
handoff as staged concepts. They do not prove charging demand, grid feasibility,
AV safety, utilization, or deployment readiness.

**Implication:** Say "relay hubs can stage charging and future automation as
evidence-backed pilots." Do not say relay hubs make EV or AV freight ready.

**Confidence:** High for claim boundary.

### ROUTE-RELAY-06 — Relay ROI is a mechanism stack, not a number.

**Source references:** `docs/reports/route-roi-cost-framework.md` lines 147-160;
`docs/briefs/funder-value-brief.md` lines 55-64.

**Observed constraint:** Relay ROI categories include crew exchange, charging,
transfer, marketplace, resilience, and state development. Each requires a cost
mechanism and evidence basis before any ROI claim.

**Implication:** Funders should be asked to support relay source packs and
pilots, not to accept positive ROI.

**Confidence:** High.

## Relay Hub Doctrine

Relay hubs are the operating nodes that make Interstate 2.0 more than pavement.

| Hub Role | Plain-English Job | Evidence Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Crew base | Let drivers hand off freight and work more predictable regional shifts. | Labor assumptions, schedule model, safety review, operator input. |
| Charging depot | Put heavy-duty charging and service where freight movement is planned. | Charging demand, grid/site readiness, dwell assumptions, utilization. |
| Transfer point | Enable trailer, load, driver, and future AV handoffs. | Yard design, dwell model, safety/operations review. |
| Marketplace node | Support earlier matching of loads, trailers, drivers, and capacity. | Carrier/shipper use cases, data governance, utilization model. |
| Resilience checkpoint | Stage freight during closures, incidents, weather, or port disruption. | Scenario evidence, recovery target, alternate capacity. |
| State asset | Create visible jobs, services, and infrastructure packages. | State delivery review, community review, local access, mitigation. |

## Safe Language

| Use This | Avoid This |
|---|---|
| "Relay hubs are the aviation model for premium freight." | "Trucking can simply become aviation." |
| "Relay hubs can stage driver exchange, charging, handoff, and future automation." | "Relay hubs solve driver shortage, EV deployment, or AV freight." |
| "A relay pilot can test a specific operating function." | "A relay network is ready to build." |
| "Driver-quality benefits are a promising hypothesis." | "Relay hubs prove safety or labor benefits." |
| "ROI starts with a source-backed evidence package." | "Relay hubs have positive ROI." |

## Pilot Concepts To Fund

| Pilot | What It Tests | What Would Promote It |
|---|---|---|
| Driver relay pilot | Whether a corridor can support scheduled driver exchange and regional shifts. | Operator participation, HOS model, schedule reliability, safety review. |
| Charging / service pilot | Whether a node can support heavy-duty charging and maintenance needs. | Grid/site evidence, dwell assumptions, utilization, maintenance owner. |
| Transfer yard pilot | Whether load/trailer handoffs can reduce dwell or improve reliability. | Yard design, dwell model, safety review, carrier use cases. |
| Resilience staging pilot | Whether a hub improves response during closure, weather, or port disruption. | Scenario evidence, recovery target, capacity assumptions. |
| State development pilot | Whether a hub can be packaged as jobs, services, access, and mitigation. | State delivery checklist, community intake, lifecycle cost scope. |

### Prototype Behind A Compatibility Boundary

| Prototype | Owner | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Relay operating model worksheet. | Freight Economist / Freight Industry | HOS, dwell, schedule, safety, and utilization assumptions named. |
| Relay hub source pack. | research owner | Charging, truck parking, maintenance, land/site, operator, and state delivery sources inventoried. |
| Relay scenario fixture. | route-sim / route-cli | Command output or scenario artifact shows a relay assumption changing a service outcome or evidence label. |

### Reject Or Defer

| Claim / Action | Reason |
|---|---|
| Claiming relay hubs solve driver shortage. | Labor assumptions and driver review are not closed. |
| Claiming EV freight readiness. | Charging demand, grid, site, and utilization evidence are not closed. |
| Claiming AV freight readiness. | Safety, regulatory, operations, and supervision assumptions are not closed. |
| Publishing positive relay ROI. | Cost, benefit, uncertainty, exclusions, and numeracy review are not closed. |
| Naming hub locations as build-ready. | Site, state delivery, environmental, community, and owner evidence are not closed. |

## Non-Goals

- This report does not select relay hub locations.
- This report does not define construction scope.
- This report does not estimate costs, ROI, utilization, labor savings, safety
  effects, charging demand, or AV readiness.
- This report does not claim state, community, industry, or agency approval.

## Gate

Decision: pass_with_risk

Rationale: Relay hubs are ready as one of the strongest Interstate 2.0 story
elements and should remain central in decks and briefs. They are not ready as
numeric ROI, labor, EV, AV, safety, utilization, or construction claims until
the named evidence gates close.
