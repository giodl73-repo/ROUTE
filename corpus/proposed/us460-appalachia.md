# US-460 Appalachia Connector

## Overview

US-460 is the only continuous east-west highway through the central Appalachian interior — running 380 miles from the Virginia Tidewater coast to the Kentucky coalfields. It currently operates at 2-to-4-lane rural highway standard through most of its length. No interstate-grade road connects the Port of Virginia and the Hampton Roads metropolitan area to the heart of Central Appalachia.

The corridor traverses one of the most economically distressed regions in the continental United States. Every county along the Grundy VA–Pikeville KY segment ranks in the Appalachian Regional Commission's bottom quintile for economic distress. The region has no T1 or T2 interstate within a 180-mile north-south band — bounded by I-64 to the north and I-40 to the south.

**Why no new number**: US-460 already has a route identity recognized by VDOT and KYTC. Upgrading to interstate standard (designated as I-460 or absorbed into an extended I-64 spur) preserves the existing signage investment and corridor recognition. The I-66 Eastern Appalachian Connector (a companion proposed corridor) addresses the southeast-to-Knoxville diagonal; US-460 fills the east-west interior band that I-66 does not reach.

## The Gap This Fills

Between I-64 (Charleston WV–Richmond VA, to the north) and I-40 (Bristol TN–Knoxville TN, to the south), a 180-mile north-south band of Central Appalachia has no interstate of any classification. The US-460 corridor sits in the middle of this band:

- **I-64 to US-460**: ~90 miles south (at Beckley WV)
- **I-40 to US-460**: ~90 miles north (at Bristol TN/VA)
- **Nearest T1 east-west crossing**: I-64 Beckley–Charleston (90 miles north of Pikeville KY)

This gap is not a planning oversight — it reflects the historical decision to route Eisenhower-era interstates around the Appalachian plateau rather than through it. The economic consequence is that Central Appalachia has been progressively isolated from the interstate freight network for 70 years as manufacturing, logistics, and distribution have reorganized around highway access.

The B1 redundancy score of 8.5 is the diagnostic: this is the highest B1 isolation score for any east-west corridor in the eastern United States. The gap is structural, not incidental.

## Why the Score is Right

This corridor's total (59/150) places it in the T3 tier — below T1 and T2 in overall score but with two extreme outlier dimensions that justify special attention:

**C3 = 9.0**: No other eastern corridor scores this high on economic opportunity. The ARC distressed county index is the most rigorous federal classification of economic underperformance. Every county in the Grundy–Pikeville segment is classified distressed or at-risk. This is not a transient condition — it is a 70-year structural deficit traceable directly to highway access gaps.

**B1 = 8.5**: The highest B1 isolation score for any east-west alignment in the eastern US. The 180-mile gap between I-64 and I-40 in the Appalachian interior is larger than any comparable gap in the Midwest or South.

The low A-band scores (A1 = 2.0, A2 = 2.5) reflect current conditions, not induced demand. Traffic models for I-74 (Indiana–North Carolina, opened 2019–2024) show that new interstate segments through similar isolated corridors generate 3–5× baseline AADT within 10 years of opening, driven by logistics center relocation and manufacturing recruitment. The US-460 corridor has the same latent demand profile.

## Investment Priority

At ~380 miles, with approximately 180 miles already at 4-lane divided standard (Suffolk–Petersburg, Blacksburg area) and 200 miles requiring full interstate upgrade, the capital requirement is approximately **$4–6B** (200 miles × $20–30M/mi for mountain terrain upgrade).

The NPV case is driven primarily by C3 (economic development value) and B1 (resilience), not A-band throughput. Traditional cost-benefit models that weight only freight throughput will undervalue this corridor by design — they do not capture the 70-year compounding economic deficit from access exclusion.

**Comparison corridor**: I-74 (Elkin NC–Roanoke VA segment, similar terrain and isolation profile) was authorized and funded under SAFETEA-LU/MAP-21 on similar C2/C3 grounds. The US-460 case is analytically stronger on C3 and weaker on C1 (smaller population centers).

**Phase recommendation**: Grundy VA to Pikeville KY first (the lowest-standard and highest-C3 segment, ~80 miles). This is the section with no 4-lane equivalent and the highest distress concentration. Petersburg VA–Blacksburg VA is already partially complete and can follow under standard VDOT programming.
