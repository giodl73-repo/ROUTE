# Iowa State Service Network Goals

## Product Goal

ROUTE helps Iowa turn roads into a service product.

The offer is not a source-audit exercise. The offer is a state service network:
which places must stay connected, what promise each tier should make, how the
network should fail gracefully, and what investment packages make those
promises credible.

Client-facing line:

> Give us your top cities, freight nodes, rural production regions, hospitals,
> universities, airports, river terminals, and industrial parks. ROUTE will
> design the state service network: tiers, promises, resilience standards,
> investment packages, and the operating story.

## Iowa Starter Network

This is the first product hypothesis to put in front of an Iowa client for
refinement.

| Tier | Iowa Role | Candidate Places |
|---|---|---|
| T1 state spine | Highest-reliability statewide and national-gateway movement | Des Moines, Cedar Rapids/Iowa City, Quad Cities, Council Bluffs/Omaha gateway, Sioux City, Waterloo/Cedar Falls |
| T2 regional connectors | Connect secondary metros, campuses, manufacturing regions, and cross-state links to the spine | Ames, Dubuque, Mason City, Fort Dodge, Marshalltown, Ottumwa, Burlington, Clinton, Muscatine |
| T3 rural and production access | Keep agricultural, manufacturing, energy, and rural-service regions from being invisible in volume-only planning | county seats, ethanol and grain corridors, food processing, wind/energy service areas, rural hospital access areas |
| T4 terminal and local access | Make the last mile work | airports, rail/intermodal yards, river terminals, industrial parks, hospitals, universities, downtown freight districts |
| R resilience overlay | Keep Iowa connected during flood, snow, bridge, work-zone, or major incident disruptions | Des Moines I-35/I-80, Mississippi and Missouri River crossings, metro bypasses, winter operations corridors, emergency detours |

## Promise Menu

These are product promises ROUTE can design for the client. They are not legal
or operational guarantees until Iowa chooses targets, assigns ownership, funds
delivery, and adopts operating rules.

| Program | Promise To Design | Top-Tier Goal |
|---|---|---|
| State spine reliability | The top Iowa city pairs and national gateways have a visible reliability standard. | A T1 network where delay, closure, and detour risk are managed as a service outcome, not just reported after failure. |
| Regional access | Every major region knows how it reaches the spine. | A T2 network that connects secondary cities, campuses, hospitals, and job centers without forcing all value through Des Moines. |
| Rural production access | Agriculture, energy, and manufacturing access are protected as economic infrastructure. | A T3 network that keeps farm-to-market, processor, energy, and rural emergency access visible in investment decisions. |
| Terminal access | The last mile to freight, airports, hospitals, campuses, and industrial parks is part of the network. | A T4 network where terminal bottlenecks, truck routing, safety conflicts, and local access gaps are named as service failures. |
| Resilience | Iowa knows which failures would break the state and which alternates must work. | A resilience overlay for flood, snow, bridge, work-zone, crash, and interchange disruptions, with recovery targets by tier. |
| Investment packaging | The state gets fundable packages instead of one giant map. | A staged program: quick operations wins, asset fixes, interchange/bypass work, terminal access, rural access, resilience hardening, and long-range capital. |
| Operating dashboard | Leaders can see whether the promise is being delivered. | Metrics for reliability, recovery, access, condition, bottlenecks, incident exposure, and package progress. |

## Service Targets To Offer

Use these as design targets in a sales conversation.

| Tier | Example Service Target |
|---|---|
| T1 | Highest-reliability movement between top Iowa metros and national gateways, with explicit incident recovery and detour standards. |
| T2 | Predictable regional access to the T1 spine, major employment centers, universities, hospitals, and freight districts. |
| T3 | Protected rural and production-zone access during harvest, winter operations, flood events, and bridge/road restrictions. |
| T4 | Reliable first/last-mile access to terminals, airports, hospitals, campuses, downtowns, and industrial parks. |
| R | Tier-specific recovery standards after closures, severe weather, work zones, bridge restrictions, or major incidents. |

## Sales Narrative

Iowa does not need another static highway map. Iowa needs a service model for
what the highway system is supposed to deliver.

ROUTE gives Iowa a way to say:

- which places must be connected,
- which connections deserve the strongest reliability promise,
- which rural and production areas must not be erased,
- which terminal and local access points make the economy work,
- which failures would be unacceptable,
- which investments are quick operational fixes versus capital programs,
- and which dashboard proves progress.

## Boundary

Do not sell this as an official Iowa DOT plan, adopted route designation,
construction program, legal SLA guarantee, funding commitment, ROI proof,
environmental clearance, right-of-way clearance, maintenance commitment, public
launch, or external validation.

Sell it as a service-network design product that can become contractual or
operational after the client selects targets, sources, ownership, funding,
delivery rules, and review process.
