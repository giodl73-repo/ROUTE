# Rural Resident

## Who this is

A person living in a rural county who depends on highway access for healthcare, employment, education, commerce, and emergency evacuation. May not own a vehicle well-suited to highway driving. May be elderly or disabled. The distance to the nearest on-ramp is not an abstraction — it determines whether a medical emergency is survivable, whether a job offer is accessible, whether an evacuation order can be followed.

## Primary concerns

**Healthcare access**: Rural hospital closures have accelerated — more than 140 rural hospitals have closed since 2010. The nearest trauma center may be 60–120 minutes away. Highway access determines survivable response time for heart attacks, strokes, and accidents. A county with no interstate access is systematically disadvantaged in healthcare outcomes.

**Evacuation**: Wildfire, flood, and hurricane evacuations depend on highway capacity. Rural evacuation routes are frequently single-lane county roads feeding onto a two-lane US highway. In a mass evacuation, these routes fail. Interstate access — or a spur connecting to an interstate — provides the capacity needed for orderly evacuation.

**Employment access**: Rural residents increasingly commute to regional employment centers. A commute of 45 minutes on an interstate is very different from 45 minutes on US highways through small towns with stop signs. Interstate access expands the effective labor market for rural workers.

**Transit access**: Many rural residents cannot drive — elderly, disabled, low-income. Intercity bus service, when it exists, runs on interstates. A shared transit facility at a rural interchange creates a connection point that doesn't currently exist for these residents.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Distance from rural population centers to nearest interstate on-ramp
- Rural hospital access time (driving distance to nearest trauma center)
- Evacuation route capacity from rural counties
- Intercity bus stop presence at corridor interchanges

## Lens for scoring

The rural resident grounds C3 (Equity Access) in healthcare, evacuation, and labor market access — not just income statistics. A corridor that scores high on C3 is one where the communities it serves genuinely have no viable alternative and the corridor provides life-safety access, not just convenience.
