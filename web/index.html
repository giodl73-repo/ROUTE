What this model can tell you

Travel uses ROUTE’s existing BPR formula at 65 mph free-flow speed, with α = 0.15 and β = 4. All three fixed legs carry the specified hourly flow continuously. This is a steady-state congestion sensitivity calculation, not route choice or a time-dependent queue simulation. Extreme demand/capacity settings can produce very large times.

The relay model applies an outage fraction to daily swaps, subtracts a reserve-driver proxy, then absorbs a fraction of the remaining swaps at adjacent hubs. “Retained” divides by outage-affected swaps, not all daily traffic. Staffing comes from ROUTE’s existing relay staffing model; these are illustrative model outputs, not employment forecasts. A zero-hour outage shows 100% retention because no swaps are affected.

Whole-trip comparison uses the existing native Monte Carlo model: traffic varies 80-120%, with a 10% surge chance; V/C is capped at 1.3 in the trip BPR calculation. Each leg has an invented 10% incident chance, with lognormal delay (mean 1 hour, standard deviation 0.5 hour). Solo drivers take 10-hour rests per 11 driving hours; team swaps take 4 minutes; two relay swaps take 20-25 minutes each. Every trip includes 2 hours for other stops. These simplified driver assumptions are not a legal hours-of-service check. Trip results exclude the separate hub outage model. They can differ from the steady-state driving card above.

The travel target measures driving time only. It excludes loading, rests, relay waits, and delivery deadlines. Road congestion and the hub outage are not coupled into a delivery simulation. The baseline always has 100% demand, 100% capacity, no outage, and the same corridor and target.

ROUTE’s I-80 research decision remains hold and narrow. This sandbox does not promote held proposals. The portable kernel is owned by ROUTE and shared with the native simulator; JavaScript only presents its results.
