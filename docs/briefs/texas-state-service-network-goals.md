# Texas State Service Network Goals

## Product Goal

ROUTE helps Texas turn a very large highway system into a service product.

The Texas offer is not a source-audit exercise and not a finished state map.
The offer is a service-network design package: which megaregions, ports,
border crossings, energy regions, rural communities, logistics nodes, and
emergency corridors must stay connected; what promise each tier should make;
how the network should fail gracefully; and what investment packages make those
promises credible.

Client-facing line:

> Give us your top metros, ports, border crossings, freight corridors, energy
> regions, rural access needs, military and emergency corridors, airports,
> rail/intermodal nodes, and unacceptable failure modes. ROUTE will design the
> Texas service network: tiers, promises, resilience standards, investment
> packages, and the operating story.

## Why Texas Is The Next Stress Test

Texas is a harder proof of ROUTE's state-client product than Iowa because the
system has to handle scale, multimodal freight, border movement, energy
production, Gulf Coast resilience, very large metros, rural access, and
fast-changing growth pressures at the same time.

| Texas Stressor | Product Implication |
|---|---|
| Megaregion scale | The network needs a hierarchy that can separate statewide spine, metro access, and cross-state movement. |
| Ports and Gulf freight | Terminal access and hurricane resilience cannot be treated as afterthoughts. |
| Border movement | Cross-border freight and passenger access need explicit service roles and review gates. |
| Energy logistics | Oil, gas, wind, petrochemical, and industrial movements require production-access visibility. |
| Rural distance | Lower-density access needs must not disappear behind metropolitan volume. |
| Emergency and evacuation | Failure modes need pre-designed alternates, recovery targets, and operational dashboards. |

## Texas Starter Network

This is the first product hypothesis to put in front of a Texas client for
refinement. It is illustrative and not an official route designation.

| Tier | Texas Role | Candidate Places Or Movements |
|---|---|---|
| T1 state spine | Highest-reliability statewide, national, border, and port movement | Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio, Austin, El Paso, Rio Grande Valley, Corpus Christi, Beaumont/Port Arthur, Laredo gateway |
| T2 megaregion connectors | Connect major metros, fast-growth corridors, campuses, military regions, and cross-state links to the spine | Austin-San Antonio, Houston-Gulf Coast, DFW-north Texas, Waco/Temple/Killeen, Tyler/Longview, Lubbock, Amarillo, Midland/Odessa |
| T3 rural and production access | Keep energy, agriculture, manufacturing, military, and rural-service regions visible in investment decisions | Permian Basin, Panhandle, Eagle Ford, rural hospitals, county seats, agricultural corridors, wind-energy service areas |
| T4 terminal and local access | Make the last mile work | Gulf ports, border crossings, airports, rail/intermodal yards, military installations, industrial parks, refineries, hospitals, universities, warehouse districts |
| R resilience overlay | Keep Texas connected during hurricanes, floods, extreme heat, winter events, wildfire, bridge restrictions, major incidents, or evacuation demand | Gulf Coast evacuation corridors, border-crossing disruption, Houston/Gulf industrial access, DFW and Austin/San Antonio bottlenecks, rural detour exposure |

## Promise Menu

These are product promises ROUTE can design with the client. They are not legal
or operational guarantees until Texas chooses targets, assigns ownership, funds
delivery, and adopts operating rules.

| Program | Promise To Design | Top-Tier Goal |
|---|---|---|
| State spine reliability | The top Texas megaregions, ports, border gateways, and national corridors have visible reliability standards. | A T1 network where delay, closure, detour, and recovery risk are managed as service outcomes. |
| Megaregion access | Fast-growth metros and regional centers know how they connect without forcing every value question through one corridor. | A T2 network that protects regional access, commuting pressure, freight movement, and public-service access. |
| Border and port access | Border crossings, Gulf ports, rail/intermodal yards, refineries, airports, and warehouse districts are part of the service network. | A T4 network where terminal bottlenecks and local access gaps are named as service failures. |
| Energy and rural production access | Energy, agriculture, petrochemical, military, and rural hospital access are protected as economic and public-service infrastructure. | A T3 network that keeps production regions and rural service areas visible in state investment choices. |
| Resilience and evacuation | Texas knows which failures would break the state service promise and which alternates must work. | A resilience overlay for hurricane, flood, winter, heat, wildfire, incident, bridge, and evacuation disruptions, with recovery targets by tier. |
| Investment packaging | The state gets fundable packages instead of one giant wish list. | A staged program: operations, incident management, freight/terminal access, rural access, resilience hardening, asset fixes, and capital programs. |
| Operating dashboard | Leaders can see whether the promise is being delivered. | Metrics for reliability, recovery, access, terminal friction, resilience exposure, asset condition, bottlenecks, and package progress. |

## Service Targets To Offer

Use these as design targets in a sales conversation.

| Tier | Example Service Target |
|---|---|
| T1 | Highest-reliability movement among Texas megaregions, national gateways, border gateways, and Gulf Coast freight anchors, with explicit incident recovery and detour standards. |
| T2 | Predictable regional access to the T1 spine, major employment centers, military regions, universities, hospitals, and freight districts. |
| T3 | Protected rural, energy, agricultural, and production-zone access during extreme weather, incident, bridge, road-restriction, or evacuation stress. |
| T4 | Reliable first/last-mile access to ports, border crossings, rail/intermodal yards, airports, refineries, warehouses, hospitals, campuses, and industrial parks. |
| R | Tier-specific recovery standards after hurricane, flood, winter, heat, wildfire, closure, work-zone, bridge, border, or major incident disruptions. |

## Sales Narrative

Texas does not need a generic highway map. Texas needs a service model for a
network that has to move people, freight, energy, emergency response, and
international trade at the same time.

ROUTE gives Texas a way to say:

- which megaregions and gateways must be connected,
- which ports and border crossings deserve explicit service roles,
- which energy and rural production areas must not be erased,
- which terminal and local access points make the economy work,
- which failures would be unacceptable during hurricane, flood, winter, heat,
  wildfire, incident, or evacuation conditions,
- which investments are quick operational fixes versus larger capital programs,
- and which dashboard tells leaders whether the promise is being delivered.

## Boundary

Do not sell this as an official Texas DOT plan, adopted route designation,
construction program, legal SLA guarantee, funding commitment, ROI proof,
environmental clearance, right-of-way clearance, maintenance commitment, public
launch, port or border-agency endorsement, or external validation.

Sell it as a service-network design product that can become contractual or
operational after the client selects targets, sources, ownership, funding,
delivery rules, review process, and agency partners.
