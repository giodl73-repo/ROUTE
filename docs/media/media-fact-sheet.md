# ROUTE Media Fact Sheet

## One-Sentence Description

ROUTE is an evidence-bounded research and tooling project that explores
Interstate 2.0: a service hierarchy for roads organized around national spine,
regional connector, feeder/access, and terminal/local-access roles.

## Short Description

ROUTE asks what the next version of the national road system should promise.
Instead of treating every interstate as one flat category, ROUTE frames roads by
the service role they play: national freight spine, regional connector,
production-zone and rural feeder, or terminal and local freight access.

ROUTE combines generated maps, evidence labels, source-custody workflows, review
records, and simulation/reporting surfaces so claims can be inspected before
they become public arguments.

## What Interstate 2.0 Means Here

Interstate 2.0 is a service-network vision, not an adopted public program.

| Tier | Role | Media-Safe Description |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | National spine | The top service layer for national timed-freight planning questions. |
| T2 | Regional connector | The regional layer that connects major markets and supports the spine. |
| T3 | Feeder/access | The access layer for production zones, smaller metros, rural regions, ports, and border areas. |
| T4 | Terminal/local access | The local freight layer for terminals, ports, rail yards, warehouses, and last-mile access. |

The time windows used in ROUTE materials are planning targets for comparing
service roles. They are not guaranteed operating commitments.

## What ROUTE Does Not Claim

- ROUTE is not an official federal, state, regional, or agency plan.
- ROUTE does not designate construction projects.
- ROUTE does not prove positive ROI for any corridor, hub, package, or standard.
- ROUTE does not guarantee service windows or freight travel times.
- ROUTE maps do not prove construction, SLA, terminal, asset, resilience, or
  environmental readiness.
- ROUTE does not claim endorsement by USDOT, FHWA, any state DOT, Port NOLA, or
  any stakeholder.
