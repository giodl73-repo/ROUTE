# I-66 Eastern Appalachian Connector

## Overview

I-66 currently exists only as a 77-mile commuter interstate in Northern Virginia (DC suburbs → Strasburg VA). The proposed Eastern Appalachian Connector would create a new I-66 corridor of an entirely different strategic character: a 560-mile southwest diagonal from the Port of Virginia at Norfolk to the I-40/I-75 interchange at Knoxville TN — the largest inland intermodal hub in the eastern Tennessee Valley.

The alignment follows the US-58 corridor (Norfolk → Bristol TN/VA) and US-421 (Bristol → Knoxville), passing through the Appalachian interior of southside Virginia and northeast Tennessee. No T1 or T2 interstate exists within 150 miles of the interior section (Bristol–Abingdon VA corridor) in the north-south direction. This is the single largest B1 gap in the eastern US.

**Why I-66**: The existing I-66 number is underused (77 miles, Virginia only). Extending the designation southwest through Appalachia gives the corridor a recognized number without requiring a new designation. Alternatively, a new designation (I-74 was absorbed into the NC-Indiana corridor; I-76 is taken; I-68 exists in Maryland/WV) could be assigned — but I-66 southwest is directionally consistent and avoids conflicts.

**Relationship to US-460**: These two corridors are complementary, not redundant. US-460 fills the east-west interior band (Suffolk–Pikeville, latitude ~37°N). I-66 fills the diagonal Port-to-Knoxville connection (latitude 36.5°–37.5°N declining southwest). They share the Port of Virginia origin but diverge immediately — US-460 goes northwest through Petersburg and Blacksburg; I-66 goes southwest through Emporia, Martinsville, and Bristol. Together they close both the east-west and diagonal interior gaps.

## The Gap This Fills

The I-66 Eastern Appalachian alignment targets the single most under-served high-C3 corridor in the eastern US:

- **North of alignment**: I-64 (Richmond VA → Charleston WV), 150+ miles north of Bristol TN/VA
- **South of alignment**: I-40 (Knoxville TN → Asheville NC → Charlotte NC), the southern boundary
- **Interior gap width**: 150 miles between I-81 (Roanoke VA–Bristol TN/VA, which itself is the closest T1) and any east-west connector in the eastern Tennessee/southside Virginia interior

The Port of Virginia (Norfolk) is the 3rd-largest container port on the East Coast by TEU volume (3.7M TEUs in 2022, Virginia Port Authority). It currently has one primary inland connection: I-64 west through Richmond and the Shenandoah Valley. I-66 would create a second inland connection, targeting the Knoxville I-40/I-75 interchange as the Midwest and Southeast distribution hub — currently accessible only via I-81 north to I-40, adding ~100 miles.

**The Knoxville terminus** at I-40/I-75 is not arbitrary: it is the junction of the primary Midwest-to-Southeast corridor (I-75 north–south) and the primary coast-to-coast southern transcontinental (I-40 east–west). Every container moving from the Port of Virginia to Memphis, Atlanta (via I-75 south), Cincinnati, or Nashville passes through or near this node. Direct I-66 access would eliminate the I-81 detour.

## Why the Score Is Right

At 73/150, I-66 sits at the boundary between T2 and T3 — the highest total among the Appalachian proposed corridors and close to the T2 threshold (~75+). Two dimensions drive this:

**C3 = 9.5**: This is the highest C3 score in the entire proposed corridor corpus. The ARC distressed classification is based on 3-year rolling averages of per-capita income, poverty rate, and unemployment — not a single snapshot. Lee County VA has held distressed status continuously since the ARC economic classification system was established. No other proposed corridor traverses this density of continuously distressed counties.

**B1 = 9.0**: The Bristol TN/VA interior gap is larger than any other B1 gap in the eastern US by north-south width. The nearest interstate (I-81) terminates at Bristol — it does not provide an east-west or diagonal connection toward Knoxville. The corridor closes a gap that has no partial substitute.

The A-band weakness (A1 = 2.5, A2 = 3.0) reflects current conditions on 2-lane US-58 and US-421. The Eastman Chemical complex at Kingsport TN is one of the largest manufacturing facilities in the southeastern US; upgrade to interstate standard would unlock direct Port of Virginia access for the site's inbound chemical feedstocks and outbound specialty chemicals — a freight flow currently routed through I-81 north to I-66 to I-64 (an inefficient dog-leg).

## Investment Priority

At ~560 miles, with approximately 200 miles at 4-lane divided standard (Norfolk → Emporia VA; Bristol → Kingsport TN; Kingsport → Knoxville) and 360 miles requiring full interstate upgrade through mountainous terrain, the capital requirement is approximately **$9–11B** (360 miles × $25–30M/mi mountain terrain upgrade).

This is the highest capital ask among the Appalachian proposed corridors but also the strongest strategic case. The combination of:
- Port of Virginia direct access (eliminates 100-mile I-81 detour for Knoxville-bound port traffic)
- Eastman Chemical Kingsport (one of the largest US chemical manufacturing sites, no direct port access today)
- Highest C3 in corpus (ARC continuously distressed counties)
- Largest B1 gap in eastern US

...produces an investment case that is qualitatively different from rural connectivity corridors built primarily on C2/C2 grounds. The Knoxville terminus ensures that freight arriving on I-66 immediately connects to the I-40/I-75 interchange — the freight distribution hub for the eastern Tennessee Valley and a direct path to Memphis, Atlanta, Cincinnati, and Nashville.

**Phase recommendation**: Bristol TN/VA → Kingsport TN → Knoxville TN first (~100 miles, partially 4-lane, connects to Knoxville intermodal immediately). This phase is shortest, lowest-terrain-cost, and delivers the I-40/I-75 connection that unlocks the port-to-Midwest freight value. The longer Galax VA–Bristol VA mountain segment (the hardest terrain, ~150 miles) follows in Phase 2. Norfolk VA → Martinsville VA (Phase 3) completes the Port of Virginia connection.
