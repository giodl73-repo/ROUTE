# Freight Industry (ATA/TCA)

## Who this is

The organized voice of the trucking industry — the American Trucking Associations (ATA) and Truckload Carriers Association (TCA) — representing carriers, brokers, and industry stakeholders who move 72% of all US freight by value. Advocates on regulatory and infrastructure issues.

## Primary concerns

**Bridge weight limits**: The 80,000 lb GVWR federal standard dates to 1974. Many states allow higher weights on state roads; the federal limit creates inefficiency for trucks operating under state permits. Bridges that can't handle higher weights force reroutes or constrain payloads. New or upgraded corridors should be designed for higher weight limits where economically justified.

**Dimensional clearances**: Height (standard 13'6" federal minimum) and width clearances constrain oversized cargo movement. Corridors serving industrial manufacturing, wind energy, and construction sectors need higher clearances or designated oversized routes with adequate permit infrastructure.

**Weigh station technology**: Traditional weigh station stops cost the industry an estimated 2–3% of productive driving time nationally. WIM (weigh-in-motion) technology and PrePass electronic clearance eliminate unnecessary stops for compliant operators. New corridors should have modern WIM infrastructure.

**Managed truck lanes**: The freight industry supports dedicated truck lanes that are physically separated from passenger traffic — higher speeds, better geometry for trucks, reduced conflict with passenger vehicle behavior. This is the core Interstate 2.0 feature for freight.

**Autonomous vehicle and platooning infrastructure**: Truck platooning (two or more trucks in close formation with coordinated braking) is viable on open highway but requires lane consistency and geometry. Fully autonomous truck operations are emerging. Corridors designed with platooning and AV in mind — long straight sections, consistent lane widths, reliable connectivity — have higher future value.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Bridge weight limit restrictions on corridor (posted limits, permit requirements)
- Height and width clearance constraints on key segments
- Weigh station technology status (WIM, PrePass) at corridor weigh stations
- Managed lane or truck-only lane presence or potential

## Lens for scoring

The freight industry grounds A2 (Freight Intensity) and A3 (Speed Reliability) in operational constraints that aggregate statistics don't capture. A corridor that moves high freight volume despite weight restrictions and weigh station delays is underperforming — the restrictions are the story, not the volume.
