# Iowa Service Network Sample Readout

## Status

This is an illustrative client readout. It shows the kind of output ROUTE can
return after an Iowa discovery workshop. It is not an Iowa DOT plan, adopted
state priority list, validated source result, legal SLA, construction program,
funding commitment, ROI proof, public-ready map, or external validation.

## Executive Readout

The workshop answer is simple:

> Iowa wants a highway service network that keeps its largest economic centers
> connected, protects rural production access, makes last-mile freight and
> hospital/campus access visible, and names resilience failures before they
> become emergencies.

ROUTE converts that answer into four working products:

| Product | What It Gives Iowa |
|---|---|
| Draft service hierarchy | A first T1/T2/T3/T4/resilience structure for discussion |
| Promise backlog | The planning promises Iowa may choose to refine by tier |
| Resilience agenda | The failures that need alternates, recovery targets, or hardening |
| 30/60/90 package | The first delivery sequence for analysis, dashboarding, and decision support |

## Sample Client Priorities

| Priority Type | Illustrative Iowa Inputs |
|---|---|
| Top cities and gateways | Des Moines, Cedar Rapids/Iowa City, Quad Cities, Council Bluffs/Omaha gateway, Sioux City, Waterloo/Cedar Falls, Ames, Dubuque |
| Freight and production | I-35/I-80 movement, Mississippi and Missouri River crossings, river terminals, rail/intermodal yards, industrial parks, food processing, grain, ethanol, wind-energy service areas |
| Rural access | County seats, rural hospitals, agricultural production corridors, winter operations corridors, farm-to-market links |
| Public-service access | Hospitals, universities, airports, emergency detours, regional medical access |
| Known stressors | Snow and ice, flooding, bridge restrictions, work zones, interchange incidents, river-crossing disruption, metro bypass friction |
| Political gaps | Regions that feel invisible in a volume-only planning process |

## Draft Service Hierarchy

| Tier | Draft Iowa Role | Sample Places Or Movements |
|---|---|---|
| T1 state spine | Highest-reliability statewide and national-gateway movement | Des Moines to Cedar Rapids/Iowa City, Quad Cities, Council Bluffs/Omaha gateway, Sioux City, Waterloo/Cedar Falls, I-35/I-80 spine movements |
| T2 regional connectors | Regional centers, campuses, hospitals, manufacturing areas, and cross-state connectors | Ames, Dubuque, Mason City, Fort Dodge, Marshalltown, Ottumwa, Burlington, Clinton, Muscatine |
| T3 rural and production access | Rural service, agricultural, food-processing, energy, and manufacturing access | county seats, ethanol and grain corridors, rural hospital access areas, wind-energy service areas |
| T4 terminal and local access | First/last-mile access where local friction breaks the larger service promise | airports, rail/intermodal yards, river terminals, industrial parks, hospitals, universities, downtown freight districts |
| R resilience overlay | Failures that require alternates, recovery expectations, or hardening | I-35/I-80 incident exposure, Mississippi/Missouri crossings, flood-prone corridors, winter operations corridors, bridge/work-zone detours |

## First Promise Backlog

These are planning targets for refinement, not adopted guarantees.

| Promise | First Draft Meaning | Next Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Top centers stay connected | Iowa's largest centers and national gateways are treated as a service system, not isolated project lists. | Select the T1 city pairs and gateway movements. |
| Freight gets reliability visibility | Freight corridors, terminals, industrial parks, and production regions get explicit bottleneck and recovery questions. | Pick the freight nodes and movements that define the first package. |
| Rural production is not erased | Agricultural, energy, processing, and rural hospital access are visible even when traffic volume is not the strongest argument. | Select the first rural/production access areas to protect. |
| Last mile counts | Terminal, airport, rail yard, hospital, campus, downtown, and industrial-park access gaps are treated as service failures. | Identify the highest-friction T4 access points. |
| Failures have alternates | Snow, flood, bridge, incident, interchange, and work-zone disruptions get route and recovery expectations by tier. | Pick the first resilience stressors for analysis. |
| Investment is staged | Operational fixes, asset fixes, access fixes, resilience hardening, and capital packages are separated. | Choose first 30/60/90-day package sequence. |

## Resilience Agenda

| Stressor | ROUTE Question | Sample Output |
|---|---|---|
| Winter operations | Which T1/T2 movements need explicit snow and ice recovery expectations? | Winter reliability target menu by tier |
| Flooding | Which river crossings and low-lying alternates must remain usable or recover quickly? | Flood detour and hardening backlog |
| Bridge restrictions | Which bridges can break regional, rural, or terminal access if restricted? | Bridge-risk watchlist for service tiers |
| Work zones | Which planned work zones require service-preserving staging? | Construction-staging service impact list |
| Major incidents | Which interchanges or metro approaches can create statewide ripple effects? | Incident alternate-route and dashboard questions |
| Terminal access failure | Which local bottlenecks make larger corridors underperform? | T4 access repair backlog |

## 30/60/90 Package

| Window | Client-Facing Output |
|---|---|
| 30 days | Confirm priority places, draft T1/T2/T3/T4/R hierarchy, and unresolved tradeoffs. |
| 60 days | Produce first promise table, resilience stressor list, alternate-route target menu, and T4 access backlog. |
| 90 days | Deliver a staged service-network package: operating fixes, asset fixes, rural/terminal access packages, resilience hardening candidates, dashboard spec, evidence gaps, and adoption options. |

## Dashboard Questions

| Dashboard Area | Question For Leaders |
|---|---|
| Reliability | Are the selected T1/T2 movements performing like the intended service tier? |
| Recovery | Which closures, incidents, or weather events recovered slower than the planning target? |
| Access | Which rural, hospital, campus, terminal, or industrial access points remain weak? |
| Resilience | Which alternates failed, became overloaded, or were not credible? |
| Investment | Which fixes are operational, which require asset work, and which belong in larger capital packages? |
| Evidence | Which claims are ready for internal decision support and which still require source validation? |

## Unresolved Tradeoffs

These are the expected hard choices after the first workshop.

| Tradeoff | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Des Moines-centered spine versus distributed regional access | A service network must support the state capital and largest movements without erasing secondary regions. |
| Freight throughput versus local/community friction | Terminal and industrial access may improve freight service while creating local safety, land-use, or community concerns. |
| Rural visibility versus budget concentration | Lower-volume production and hospital access needs may compete with higher-volume metro bottlenecks. |
| Resilience hardening versus near-term operations | Some fixes may be cheap operating changes; others may require asset or capital programs. |
| Dashboard simplicity versus operational truth | Leaders need a simple view, but operations teams need enough detail to manage service failures. |

## What To Do Next

ROUTE should use this readout to drive a second client session:

1. Confirm or revise the sample priority list.
2. Choose the first T1/T2/T3/T4/R definitions.
3. Select the first three unacceptable failures.
4. Pick one freight, one rural, one terminal, and one resilience package.
5. Decide which dashboard questions matter for executive review.
