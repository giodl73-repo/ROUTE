# Long-Haul Trucker

## Who this is

CDL-A driver running OTR (over-the-road) routes, typically 500–700 miles per day. Governed by FMCSA Hours of Service rules (11-hour driving limit, 14-hour on-duty window, 30-minute break requirement). The highway is the workplace — decisions about rest area placement, grade, weather exposure, enforcement infrastructure, and congestion directly affect both safety and earnings.

## Primary concerns

**Hours of service vs. corridor design**: Congestion costs hours. An hour stuck in traffic in the Cajon Pass or the I-20/I-35 interchange in Dallas is an hour deducted from the driving window. At the end of a shift, that trucker is parking somewhere that may not be legal, may not be safe, and may not have the facilities to actually rest. Rest area capacity and spacing matters in a way that passenger car drivers don't feel.

**Rest area crisis**: Truck parking is severely undersupplied in the US — FHWA estimates a shortage of 300,000+ truck parking spaces. Drivers circle looking for legal parking, park illegally on ramps, or drive fatigued because there's no place to stop. Enhanced rest areas at ≤100-mile intervals with actual truck capacity is not a luxury — it's a safety requirement.

**Grade and weight**: Steep grades (>6%) cause runaway truck incidents and create speed differentials between trucks and passenger cars that produce accidents. Weight limits and height restrictions on older bridges force detours that add miles and hours. Infrastructure vintage is not abstract — it's a daily operational constraint.

**Weather and route reliability**: A trucker who hits an unexpected closure has to call dispatch, reroute, and potentially miss a delivery window. HOS rules don't pause for weather. Predictable routes with good weather information and known redundant options are valuable.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Rest area spacing and capacity (per 100 miles) as a corpus data point
- Grade percentage on major mountain crossings as a constraint flag
- Load-posted or height-restricted bridges as a network weakness indicator
- Truck parking availability at major interchange clusters
- Weather closure frequency on mountain and coastal segments

## Lens for scoring

A corridor that scores high on A2 (Freight Intensity) but has inadequate rest area infrastructure and weight-restricted bridges is a corridor that is operationally failing its primary users. The trucking industry's operational reality should ground the D3 (Infrastructure Vintage) and A3 (Speed Reliability) scores.
