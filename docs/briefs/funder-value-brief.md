# Interstate 2.0 Funder Value Brief

## The Funder Pitch

Interstate 2.0 is not asking funders to approve a finished national build map.
It is asking funders to back the next planning layer the country is missing:

```text
service promise -> requirements intake -> evidence package -> pilots -> fundable options
```

The value is that states, industry, communities, and evidence can refine the
plan before major construction commitments are made.

## What To Fund First

| Package | What It Buys | Why It Is Safe |
|---|---|---|
| Story package | Public Interstate 2.0 deck, map visuals, state/industry/funder briefs. | Builds alignment without claiming construction readiness. |
| Requirements intake | State DOT, freight, rural, community, environmental, and funder input process. | Turns stakeholders into plan inputs before positions harden. |
| Evidence package | Source acquisition, stop/SLA artifacts, map diagnostics, claim labels, role review. | Separates story-ready claims from heuristic, held, and source-needed claims. |
| Demo package | A reproducible requirement-to-refinement runbook and artifact path. | Shows how requirements change evidence posture before promising outcomes. |
| Pilot package | Candidate hubs, charging/staging studies, interchange studies, access reviews, resilience tests. | Keeps early funding tied to studies, pilots, and decision gates. |
| ROI/cost source pack | Price-year rules, benefit/cost classes, uncertainty, exclusions, and review lanes. | Blocks fake ROI numbers while preparing real benefit-cost work. |

## Why This Is A Better Ask

| Traditional Megaproject Ask | Interstate 2.0 Ask |
|---|---|
| "Approve this map." | "Fund the standard and evidence layer that makes maps testable." |
| "Trust the ranking." | "Inspect the artifacts, assumptions, and role tensions." |
| "Benefits will follow." | "Define benefit classes, cost classes, uncertainty, and blockers first." |
| "Stakeholders can comment later." | "Requirements enter before the plan hardens." |
| "One big construction number." | "Stage story, evidence, pilots, and decision packages." |

## What Funders Can Say

| Message | Safe Wording |
|---|---|
| National vision | "Interstate 2.0 gives roads a service hierarchy leaders can explain." |
| Responsible funding | "The first ask is evidence, standards, intake, and pilots, not a blank construction check." |
| ROI discipline | "No numeric ROI claim moves forward until price year, sources, uncertainty, and exclusions are visible." |
| Stakeholder trust | "States, industry, rural regions, and communities can change the plan before it becomes a project list." |
| Innovation | "Relay hubs can stage driver quality, charging, handoff, and future automation as evidence-backed pilots." |
| Evidence | "Promise windows are planning targets until reliability and delivery evidence close." |

## What Not To Say Yet

- This proves any corridor, hub, interchange, or standard should be built.
- This is an official federal or state plan.
- This guarantees service windows.
- This proves positive ROI or a benefit-cost ratio.
- This solves right-of-way, environmental, labor, EV, AV, safety, or delivery
  risk.
- This means stakeholders have validated the plan.

## Funder Questions The Package Should Answer

| Funder Question | Current Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the big idea? | A national road service hierarchy: spine, regional connector, feeder, terminal access. |
| Why now? | Freight reliability, resilience, EV/AV transition, driver quality, rural access, and staged infrastructure need a common operating frame. |
| Why not just fund construction? | Construction claims are premature until requirements, evidence, delivery, and stakeholder gates are visible. |
| What is the near-term product? | Decks, briefs, generated maps, stop/SLA artifacts, diagnostics, ROI/cost framework, and a demo runbook. |
| What changes when new requirements arrive? | ROUTE can turn requirements into thresholds, diagnostics, candidate dockets, holds, and evidence asks. |
| What would make this investment-ready? | Source-backed reliability, delivery, cost, role-review, and scenario evidence for a named package. |

## A Responsible Funding Sequence

1. **Vision:** make the national service promise legible.
2. **Intake:** collect state, industry, rural, community, environmental, and
   delivery requirements.
3. **Evidence:** generate artifacts and label every claim.
4. **Pilots:** test hubs, access, charging, resilience, and interchange packages.
5. **Decision:** promote, hold, downgrade, or reject options with sources and
   review records.

## Role Review Notes

| Role Lens | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Scope Keeper | Brief asks funders to support planning, evidence, intake, and pilots; it does not prescribe construction. | pass |
| Numeracy Checker | No ROI, cost, traffic, benefit, or schedule numbers are introduced. | pass |
| Citation Auditor | Claims trace to repo-local decks, evidence posture, ROI/cost framework, and demo docs. External source claims remain gated. | pass_with_risk |
| Freight Economist | Funding ask is tied to reliability, benefit/cost evidence, and negative-case discipline rather than fake ROI. | pass_with_risk |
| State DOT Planner | Delivery, match, maintenance, right-of-way, and agency authority remain visible. | pass_with_risk |
| Stakeholder Review | Community, rural, environmental, and industry requirements are first-class intake lanes. | pass_with_risk |

## Gate

Decision: pass_with_risk

Rationale: This brief is suitable as an internal funder-facing leave-behind. It
keeps the ask at the story, evidence, intake, demo, pilot, and ROI-source level.
It remains blocked from corridor-specific, construction, official-plan,
guaranteed-SLA, or numeric ROI claims until source evidence and role review close
for a concrete package.
