# ROUTE — Board Review

## Executive Summary

The ROUTE module is a significant research contribution in a field that is underserved by quantitative analysis. The US interstate system has not been systematically scored against a calibrated multi-dimensional rubric at the corpus level. ROUTE does that. The resulting findings — a 4-tier hierarchy, 15 missing or underbuilt links, compound resilience holes, a $6.2B/yr closure cost estimate, and a $253B upgrade specification — are the kind of numbers that a Congressional Budget Office analyst, an FHWA program officer, or a Senate Transportation Committee staffer could actually use.

The module is **not yet ready for external release**. Four PP1 blocking items remain open (E.2 NPV arithmetic gap, F.1/F.2 proximity-access conflation, D.1 composite metric units, C.1 BPR extrapolation). After these are addressed, the module is expected to score 8.2/10 — publishable and impactful.

**Unique contribution**: No other publicly available analysis scores the full US interstate corpus (227 corridors) against a calibrated multi-dimensional rubric at corridor level with public data. FHWA publishes performance measures by corridor, but not a synthesized tier classification. The ROUTE tier classification + gap map is genuinely new.

## What Changes If This Module Is Right

The board applies the "decision it changes" test to the module as a whole:

**Finding 1 — The tier hierarchy is real, not arbitrary.** If true: FHWA should allocate NHPP funding using betweenness-adjusted tier classifications, not state-by-state apportionment. The 8 T1 Primary Arteries should receive dedicated program lines. This would require a statutory change to federal highway apportionment formulas — a significant policy action. *Confidence: High. The congestion-stress paradox is accepted by all reviewers.*

**Finding 2 — Donner Pass is a national SPOF for northern transcontinental freight.** If true: the $4B Donner freight tunnel is the single highest-NPV infrastructure investment the federal government could make in the next decade, exceeding most rail proposals on cost-benefit grounds. *Confidence: High on the physical fact; medium on the NPV (waiting-cost rate correction pending).*

**Finding 3 — I-69 completion has measurable national impact.** If true: I-69 is not a regional project — it adds 18% Gulf-to-Midwest max-flow and eliminates the Dallas interchange as a national bottleneck. The investment case is stronger than the regional economic development case alone. *Confidence: Medium. Single-commodity max-flow may overstate the gain; multi-commodity sensitivity needed.*

**Finding 4 — The I2.0 transit layer is nearly free.** If true: the political economy of highway investment shifts. $2B in hub investments on a $253B program unlocks intercity bus connectivity for 12.4M transit-dependent Americans (proximity, not operational access — pending PP1.2 correction). The transit layer cannot be added cheaply after the fact if hub infrastructure is not designed for passenger access from day one. *Confidence: High on the cost efficiency finding; medium on the 12.4M figure pending reframing.*

**Finding 5 — Gulf Coast I-10 is the most urgent climate adaptation investment by 2040.** If true: FHWA's PROTECT program grant criteria should weight consecutive SFHA miles more heavily — the Louisiana corridor's D1 score of 9.1 by 2050 (vs. 8.4 today) makes it the most exposed piece of critical infrastructure in the continental US. *Confidence: Medium. Projection methodology pending PP1.3 validation.*
