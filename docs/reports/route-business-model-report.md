# ROUTE Business Model Report

## Thesis

ROUTE should start as a consultative product, not as pure SaaS.

The first sellable package is a fixed-scope service-network design engagement:
the buyer brings priority places, gateways, production regions, service
pressures, and unacceptable failures; ROUTE returns a tiered service hierarchy,
planning-promise backlog, resilience agenda, investment sequence, dashboard
specification, and evidence boundaries.

## Recommended Model

| Stage | Product | Buyer Commitment | ROUTE Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paid 90-day diagnostic | Fixed-fee scoped package | Discovery workshop, tier model, promise backlog, resilience blueprint, investment sequence, dashboard spec |
| 2 | Follow-on advisory retainer | Quarterly or annual support | Scenario refreshes, executive readouts, package updates, evidence gates, partner coordination |
| 3 | Repeatable product templates | Sector-specific playbooks | Turnpike, port, border, freight-corridor, state DOT, MPO, and industrial-zone packages |
| 4 | Platform / SaaS | Subscription only after workflow repeats | Hosted scenario surfaces, dashboard specs, evidence status, readout generation, package versioning |

Do not lead with SaaS. A dashboard subscription too early puts ROUTE in the
wrong competitive set against traffic analytics, GIS, and performance
management platforms. ROUTE's early value is the productized decision package.

## Buyer Priority

Start where service promises already sound natural.

| Buyer Type | Why They Fit | First Offer |
|---|---|---|
| Toll road / turnpike operator | Already thinks in customers, toll revenue, incident recovery, maintenance, and service quality. | Corridor service promise and recovery-package diagnostic |
| Port authority / inland port / terminal district | Cares about access, bottlenecks, truck flow, resilience, and land-side reliability. | Terminal-access service network and failure-mode readout |
| Private toll concession / managed-lane operator | Cares about demand, reliability, lifecycle obligations, and contractual performance. | Managed-corridor promise and evidence-bound investment package |
| Freight corridor coalition | Needs a story that ties industrial users, states, MPOs, and funding asks together. | Freight service hierarchy and staged investment package |
| State DOT innovation / planning office | Needs cross-program synthesis without replacing official authority. | 90-day state service-network package |
| MPO / regional freight body | Needs local priorities translated into state and national service roles. | Regional-to-state service role package |
| Industrial / logistics park developer | Needs access, workforce, freight, safety, and resilience claims without overpromising. | Site-to-network access promise package |

## Government Versus Private Entry

ROUTE can serve both public and private buyers, but the entry motion differs.

| Channel | Advantage | Risk | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private / authority / concession | Faster buying motion, clearer customer-service language, narrower network, revenue focus. | Smaller scope may limit statewide proof. | First paid wedge and proof of product value. |
| State DOT / MPO / public agency | Bigger market legitimacy, direct planning relevance, policy influence. | Slower procurement, more legal and political caution, stronger non-claim needs. | Second wave after two or three narrower wins. |
| Consultant / data-platform partner | Existing relationships and implementation capacity. | ROUTE can be buried as a subcontracted method. | Channel partner when scope is protected. |
| Investor / infrastructure sponsor | Clear business narrative around service-network due diligence. | May push ROI and project-readiness claims too early. | Use only with strict evidence-contract framing. |

## Why Private Infrastructure Is A Strong Wedge

Private and authority-operated transportation assets already behave more like
service products than ordinary public road networks.

| Example Class | Service-Network Relevance | ROUTE Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Brightline-style private rail | Passenger service is sold as a customer product with station access, reliability, trip time, and expansion tradeoffs. | ROUTE can evaluate road/rail access, station catchments, resilience, and service promise interfaces. |
| Brightline West-style corridor | A new intercity corridor must justify service, access, interfaces, and resilience before operation. | ROUTE can structure pre-opening promise, access, and dashboard packages without claiming ridership proof. |
| Florida Turnpike-style toll network | Public authority, toll-funded, customer-facing, operations-heavy, incident-sensitive. | ROUTE can define corridor promises, recovery expectations, investment sequence, and executive dashboards. |
| SH 130-style concession | Concession agreements tie development, finance, operation, and maintenance to a corridor business case. | ROUTE can create evidence-bound service and risk packages before expansion, refinancing, or operating review. |
| Managed lanes | Pricing and reliability are directly connected to customer experience. | ROUTE can define promise tiers, incident recovery, access conflicts, and package tradeoffs. |

## Pricing Posture

Do not publish pricing yet. Use package bands internally until value and sales
cycle are tested.

| Package | Internal Price Logic | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow corridor diagnostic | Small fixed fee | One corridor, terminal, turnpike segment, managed lane, or access district. |
| Authority / port / MPO package | Mid fixed fee | Multiple nodes, failure modes, and dashboard questions. |
| State service-network package | Larger fixed fee | Full T1/T2/T3/T4/R hierarchy and cross-program package. |
| Annual advisory | Retainer | Quarterly refresh, scenario updates, evidence gate maintenance, executive readouts. |
| Platform subscription | Later | Only after repeatable workflows and common artifacts are proven. |

The first commercial aim is not maximum contract value. It is proof that buyers
will pay for the 90-day service-network package.

## Sales Motion

| Step | Goal | Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sector-specific pitch | Make the buyer recognize the service-network pain. | Turnpike, port, border, freight, or state pitch. |
| 2. Discovery workshop | Extract priority places, service pressures, and unacceptable failures. | Workshop worksheet. |
| 3. Sample readout | Show what ROUTE returns before the buyer commits to a larger program. | Illustrative readout. |
| 4. Paid diagnostic | Deliver the first real package. | 90-day package. |
| 5. Renewal / expansion | Keep the promise model alive as sources and projects evolve. | Retainer or platform path. |

## What ROUTE Sells

ROUTE sells:

- a structured intake for priority places and unacceptable failures,
- a tiered service-network model,
- planning promises with legal-SLA holds,
- resilience failure-mode design,
- investment package sequencing,
- dashboard specification,
- evidence and non-claim discipline,
- a repeatable readout format buyers can use with leadership.

ROUTE does not initially sell:

- official route designation,
- engineering design,
- construction readiness,
- public approval,
- legal SLA guarantee,
- numeric ROI,
- funding eligibility,
- traffic-data replacement,
- toll-demand forecast replacement.

## Competitive Avoidance

ROUTE should not fight data vendors, GIS vendors, engineering firms, or DOT
planning authority.

| Incumbent Lane | ROUTE Posture |
|---|---|
| Traffic analytics / OD data | Input or partner, not replacement. |
| GIS / asset systems | Evidence and visualization surfaces, not the product core. |
| Engineering consultants | Downstream implementers or channel partners. |
| Grant consultants | Later-stage package support. |
| DOT planning teams | Authority owners and potential buyers. |
| Toll operators | Early buyers for narrower service-network packages. |

## Best Initial Wedge

The strongest first wedge is:

> A 90-day service-network diagnostic for toll roads, turnpikes, ports, managed
> lanes, freight corridors, and state DOT innovation teams.

This wedge is narrow enough to sell, but broad enough to prove the product.

## Boundary

This report is a business-model hypothesis. It does not claim paying customers,
procurement eligibility, market share, investor readiness, agency endorsement,
private-operator endorsement, legal-SLA readiness, traffic-demand proof,
numeric ROI, funding eligibility, public-readiness, or external validation.
