# I-29 Extended South — Great Plains Spine

## Overview

The existing I-29 runs Pembina ND (Canada) → Fargo → Sioux City → Kansas City MO (1,499 miles). Extending it southward through Kansas, Nebraska, and Texas to the Mexican border would create the US interstate system's first **true Canada-to-Mexico corridor on a single consistent Great Plains alignment**.

The alignment follows US-83 from Laredo/Corpus Christi north through the Texas Hill Country and Panhandle, through Dodge City KS, through central Nebraska, connecting to the existing I-29 alignment near Kansas City.

**Why I-29 extended** (vs new I-33): The existing I-29 already serves exactly this geographic function — it's the Great Plains Canada connector. Extending it south to Mexico gives it its strategic purpose. An I-33 would duplicate the concept with a new number when rationalization is cleaner.

## Strategic Case

1. **Great Plains grain spine**: The corridor bisects the heart of US wheat, corn, and sorghum production. Kansas is the largest US wheat producer; Nebraska is top-5 corn; North Dakota is top spring wheat. A direct interstate connection from these production areas to both Corpus Christi (Gulf export) and Pembina ND (Canada) is the missing infrastructure for agricultural export logistics.

2. **Eagle Pass alternative**: Laredo (on I-35) is the #1 US-Mexico crossing but Eagle Pass (on US-83/this corridor) is the 4th-largest and fastest growing. An I-29 extension would give Eagle Pass direct interstate access northward without routing through I-35/I-35's congestion.

3. **Canada trade**: Canada's Prairie Provinces (Saskatchewan, Manitoba) export grain south through the Pembina ND crossing. The existing I-29 serves Pembina. Extending south through the US grain belt creates a complete supply chain corridor.

4. **Differentiation from I-31**: I-31 (US-287 alignment) serves the western part of the central plains (Amarillo → Denver → Wyoming). I-29 extended serves the eastern part (Dodge City → Wichita → Kansas City). Together they provide two parallel Mexico-Canada corridors through the vast central plains — appropriate given the geographic width.
