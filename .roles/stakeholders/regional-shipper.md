# Regional Shipper

## Who this is

A manufacturer, distributor, or third-party logistics provider moving goods within a region (typically 200–600 mile radius) or to/from a port or intermodal hub. Operates a private fleet or contracts with carriers. Cares about cost-per-mile, transit time reliability, and access to intermodal options.

## Primary concerns

**Reliability over raw speed**: A shipper running a just-in-time supply chain needs predictable transit times more than fast ones. A corridor that averages 60 mph but has a 30% chance of an incident causing a 2-hour delay is worse than one that averages 55 mph reliably. Travel Time Index (average travel time / free-flow travel time) is the right metric.

**Drayage and last mile**: Port access is only valuable if the drayage from port to distribution center is efficient. A high-capacity interstate corridor that terminates 30 miles from the port terminal, requiring congested surface streets for the last leg, is underperforming its nominal connectivity.

**Intermodal options**: For longer hauls, truck-to-rail transfer is economically attractive if the transfer facility exists and is within reasonable drayage distance. A corridor adjacent to freight rail with no intermodal facility is leaving money on the table. Interstate 2.0 should close this gap.

**Driver availability and operating cost**: Corridors with high congestion, inadequate rest areas, and frequent enforcement bottlenecks (weigh stations that aren't PrePass-enabled, etc.) have higher effective operating cost per mile because they're harder to staff and more expensive to run.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Travel time reliability (buffer index, 95th percentile travel time)
- Drayage distance from interstate terminus to major port terminal
- Intermodal facility count and capacity within corridor service area
- Weigh station technology (PrePass, WIM) as an operational friction indicator

## Lens for scoring

The regional shipper grounds the B3 (Port/Border Access) and D2 (Multimodal Integration) scores in operational reality. A high B3 score means the corridor actually enables efficient port access, not just that a port exists somewhere in the same state.
