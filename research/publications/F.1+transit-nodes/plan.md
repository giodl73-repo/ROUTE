# Plan: T1/T1 as Transit Nodes — The Interstate 2.0 Passenger Layer

**Track**: F — Transit Integration
**Venue**: Transportation Research Part A
**Target**: 8,000–10,000 words

## The Question

The Interstate 2.0 highway investment — $209B over 30 years — is framed as a freight program. But every T1/T1 diamond hub, every C-D road, and every managed freight lane also creates the physical conditions for intercity bus transit to operate at a level of reliability that currently only exists in the Northeast Corridor. How much transit coverage does the I2.0 highway program unlock, and at what marginal cost?

## The Method

1. **Hub siting**: identify the 9 T1/T1 diamond hub locations and 50+ T1/T2 transit stops from the intersection analysis. Compute which population centers are within 20, 30, and 50 miles of each hub.
2. **Transit-dependent population**: Census ACS table B08201 (no-vehicle households) + rural population with no existing transit access → quantify who the hub network serves.
3. **Hub cost**: estimate incremental hub investment (bus platforms, parking, shelters) on top of the diamond highway investment. Compare to standalone transit infrastructure (new bus terminal in a city = $20–50M; hub increment = $2–5M per T1/T1 node).
4. **Coverage efficiency**: N transit-dependent travelers served per $M of hub investment vs. standalone transit.

## The Finding (hypothesis)

- 9 T1/T1 hubs + 50 T1/T2 stops bring X million transit-dependent Americans within 30 miles of an intercity transit connection they currently don't have
- Hub increment investment: ~$2B on a $209B highway program (1% of total cost)
- This is 10–20× more cost-efficient per traveler-served than equivalent standalone bus terminal construction
- City pairs newly connected by T1 bus routes that have no current rail or viable bus service: ~40

## Key Claims

- C1: 9 T1/T1 hubs serve a combined transit-shed of N million transit-dependent residents, at hub increment cost of $2B (1% of total I2.0 investment)
- C2: 40+ city pairs gain first-time intercity bus connectivity via T1 hub network
- C3: Transit hub marginal cost per traveler-served is 10–20× lower than standalone terminal construction
- C4: T1 hub locations align disproportionately with communities scoring high on C3 (Economic Opportunity Access) — the equity benefit of I2.0 is larger than the freight analysis alone suggests
