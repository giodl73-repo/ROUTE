# Local Official

## Who this is

A mayor, county commissioner, or regional planning director in a community along or near a corridor. Responsible for land use, economic development, and public services. May be advocating for a new interchange, a new corridor, or an upgrade — or may be opposing a proposed alignment that would divide a community.

## Primary concerns

**Economic development**: Interstate access is the single largest determinant of industrial site selection for distribution and manufacturing. An interchange with sufficient right-of-way for a truck-capable facility is worth millions in local tax base. A proposed corridor that bypasses a county entirely is a development disadvantage that compounds over decades.

**Interchange spacing and design**: Interchanges are economic assets. Their spacing, design, and access control determine whether they generate development or just move traffic. Local officials advocate for interchanges; FHWA and state DOTs control access.

**Community division**: A highway through a community severs neighborhoods, generates noise and pollution, and can depress property values in adjacent areas. Where a corridor goes — through vs. around a community — has permanent consequences for the community's social fabric.

**Funding and maintenance burden**: Federal interstates come with federal maintenance support, but the first/last mile connections, local streets around interchanges, and drainage infrastructure are often local responsibility. A new corridor that generates traffic but no accompanying local infrastructure investment is a mixed blessing.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Interchange density and spacing as an economic access indicator
- Community feedback on proposed alignments (where documented in EIS or planning records)
- Local economic development potential at proposed interchange locations
- History of community impacts from existing corridors (documented in historic records)

## Lens for scoring

The local official grounds C1 (Population Reach) and C3 (Equity Access) in specific community outcomes. High population reach that is distributed equitably across communities of different income levels scores higher than reach concentrated in affluent suburbs.
