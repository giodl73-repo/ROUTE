## Overall

The strategic dimension additions (A4, B4, C4) are the paper's substantive contribution, and they are conceptually well-founded — USMCA corridor designation, STRAHNET access, and agricultural export connectivity are all legitimate strategic functions that aggregate congestion metrics cannot capture. The freight-economic concern is narrower: A4 is the strongest of the three, C4 is plausible, but B4 (Military/Strategic) conflates two distinct freight functions — peacetime logistics and mobilization capacity — and scores them on a single dimension without separating their policy implications.

## What Works

**A4's categorical structure is correct.** The decision to assign A4 = 0 to non-border corridors rather than a continuous near-border score is defensible on freight-economic grounds. The USMCA trade function of I-35 at Laredo is qualitatively distinct from any inland corridor's trade function — I-35 is not "somewhat more trade-connected" than I-80; it physically handles a categorically different freight stream. The categorical design prevents inappropriate partial-credit inflation.

**I-35 A4 = 8.5 anchor is empirically grounded.** The Laredo port figures ($277B annual trade value, 16,000 commercial vehicles/day) are from FHWA and CBP sources and are within the range I would cite. The 0.5 deduction from 10.0 for the northern segment dilution is honest — the strategic function is concentrated at the terminus, not distributed along the 1,568-mile corridor.

**C4 Corn Belt logic is sound.** I-80's C4 = 7.5 reflects genuine agricultural export connectivity: the corridor connects Nebraska and Iowa production to both Bay Area Pacific terminals and Gulf access via I-29/I-35 junctions. The dual-coast export reach is a real supply chain property, not an artifact of the scoring design.

**Forward-only protocol protects A.1's evidentiary chain.** From a freight economics standpoint, the concern is always whether published results can be reproduced. The protocol's citation stability criterion (A.1 scores must remain reproducible from the corpus database) is the right anchor. Score retroaction that invalidates published citations is scientific misconduct; the forward-only protocol prevents it without requiring infinite rubric conservatism.

## What Doesn't Work

**B4 conflates peacetime logistics and strategic mobilization.** STRAHNET designation covers all interstates for civilian defense logistics — the 5.0 baseline reflects normal military freight (PX resupply, equipment rotation, personnel transport) that any T1 or T2 corridor handles. The additional scoring for ICBM field proximity (Malmstrom AFB, FE Warren AFB) is about a qualitatively different function: strategic force access in a crisis. These two functions have different investment implications. Peacetime military logistics looks like any other heavy freight demand and can be addressed with normal capacity investments. Strategic force access may require hardening, redundancy, and geometric standards (load limits, overhead clearances) that have nothing to do with congestion relief. Conflating them in a single 0-10 dimension masks whether the investment case is civilian or defense-driven. At minimum, B4 should be documented with the two-component decomposition explicit in the text.

**The $277B Laredo trade figure needs a vintage and methodology note.** The paper cites FHWA freight data (2023) but does not note that USMCA trade values fluctuate substantially year-to-year with commodity prices (energy, auto parts, electronics are all top categories). The 2023 figure benefits from post-pandemic trade normalization and may not represent the 5-year average. This is not a fatal problem — the Laredo crossing's dominance is structurally robust — but a TRR reviewer will want the methodology note.

**C4 hand-curation creates a reproducibility gap.** The paper acknowledges that C4 uses "hand-curated" USDA county data, with programmatic USDA ERS integration deferred to v1.3. From a freight economics standpoint, county-level agricultural production data is publicly available from USDA NASS (census of agriculture, quinquennial) and USDA ERS. The hand-curation should not be necessary for the anchor scores — the data exists at county resolution. The paper should either provide the anchor derivation from NASS/ERS in an appendix, or explicitly note that the C4 scores are provisional pending the v1.3 programmatic integration.

## The Question I'd Push On

The paper validates A4 (USMCA), B4 (military), and C4 (agricultural export) as independent dimensions capturing genuinely distinct freight-strategic functions. But the policy implications of strategic importance depend on which function is being served. A corridor scoring high on A4 calls for border inspection capacity and intermodal connectivity investment. One scoring high on C4 calls for pavement load capacity and harvest-season surge planning. One scoring high on B4 (military access) might call for DoD cost-sharing rather than FHWA programming. Does the rubric aggregate these into a single tier classification in a way that loses the investment-type signal? If I-35 is a T1 corridor primarily because of A4, its investment needs differ from I-80's T1 status driven by C4, even though both receive the same tier designation.
