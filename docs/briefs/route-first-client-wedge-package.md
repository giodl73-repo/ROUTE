# ROUTE First Client Wedge Package

## Purpose

This brief turns the business-model and competitive-positioning reports into a
first-client selection package.

The first buyer should not be the largest possible government program. The
first buyer should be a narrow operator, authority, coalition, or sponsor with
a real service-network pain, enough existing data to make a readout useful, and
enough urgency to pay for a fixed 90-day diagnostic.

## Best First-Client Profile

| Rank | Segment | Why It Fits | First Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Toll road / turnpike corridor operator | Customer-service language, revenue exposure, incident recovery, lifecycle maintenance, and corridor identity already exist. | Corridor promise, failure-mode, recovery, and investment-sequence diagnostic. |
| 2 | Port authority or inland port district | Land-side access, truck staging, terminal reliability, resilience, and public/private coordination are visible pains. | Terminal-access service network and failure-mode readout. |
| 3 | Managed-lane or private concession operator | Reliability, pricing, demand risk, operations, and contractual performance create a natural promise discussion. | Managed-corridor promise and risk-package diagnostic. |
| 4 | Freight corridor coalition | Multiple jurisdictions need a shared story without pretending to have one official plan. | Freight service hierarchy and staged investment package. |
| 5 | State DOT innovation or planning office | Big legitimacy and direct planning relevance, but slower procurement and stronger public-claim discipline. | State service-network package after a narrow proof. |

## Selection Test

Use this screen before spending time on a prospect.

| Question | Pass Signal | Hold Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Do they own or convene a corridor, gateway, terminal, or state network? | They can name priority places and unacceptable failures. | They only want a generic innovation demo. |
| Do they already use traffic, GIS, asset, incident, toll, freight, or planning data? | ROUTE can package existing evidence into a service readout. | They expect ROUTE to create all source data from scratch. |
| Do they care about service quality, reliability, access, resilience, or investment sequence? | They talk about outcomes and failures. | They only ask for a map or branding story. |
| Can they buy or sponsor a fixed diagnostic? | There is a budget owner, authority partner, or paid pilot path. | They can only offer informal feedback. |
| Will they accept evidence boundaries? | They understand legal SLA, ROI, construction, funding, and endorsement holds. | They demand guaranteed numbers before the diagnostic. |

## First Meeting Ask

The first meeting should not ask the buyer to adopt ROUTE. It should ask for a
bounded discovery session.

Ask:

> Give us your top places to connect, your unacceptable service failures, your
> existing data surfaces, and your leadership questions. We will return a
> service-network package that separates candidate promises from evidence holds.

The meeting should close on one of three paths:

| Path | Use When | Next Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic candidate | Buyer has a bounded corridor, port, managed lane, or state network question. | 90-day scope sheet. |
| Workshop only | Buyer has interest but no paid path yet. | Discovery worksheet and sample readout. |
| Hold | Buyer wants guaranteed ROI, official route designation, construction proof, or vendor replacement. | Non-fit note and claim-boundary response. |

## Discovery Inputs

Ask the buyer for:

- top cities, gateways, terminals, corridors, production zones, or districts,
- unacceptable failures, such as closure, delay, staging, incident recovery, or access gaps,
- existing traffic, toll, incident, asset, GIS, freight, safety, or planning sources,
- current dashboards or executive reports,
- planned investments or unresolved project lists,
- resilience shocks that leadership worries about,
- what they are not allowed to claim yet.

## 90-Day Output

The first paid diagnostic should return:

| Output | Buyer Value |
|---|---|
| Service hierarchy | Shows which links and nodes play T1/T2/T3/T4/R roles. |
| Promise backlog | Turns vague goals into candidate planning promises with legal-SLA holds. |
| Failure-mode ledger | Names the failures that break the promise before pretending to solve them. |
| Evidence boundary table | Separates source-backed, heuristic, held, and next-proof claims. |
| Investment package sequence | Groups projects or actions by service role and failure mode. |
| Dashboard specification | Defines what leadership should track without claiming a finished platform. |
| Executive readout | Gives the buyer a board, authority, sponsor, or leadership-facing story. |

## First-Call Script

Use this short opener:

> ROUTE is not trying to replace your traffic data, GIS, planning models, or
> engineering consultants. It gives leadership a service-network decision
> package: what the network is supposed to do, which promises are candidates,
> which failures matter most, which investments belong together, and which
> claims are not ready yet.

Then ask:

1. What are the top places or corridors you must connect reliably?
2. What service failure would embarrass leadership if it happened next quarter?
3. Which data or reports already exist, even if they are scattered?
4. What decision do you need to make before anyone asks for construction, ROI,
   funding, or legal SLA proof?

## Non-Fit Responses

| Prospect Demand | Response |
|---|---|
| "Show us the ROI number first." | ROUTE can define the ROI evidence contract, but it will not invent a number before costs, benefits, price year, uncertainty, and excluded benefits are reviewed. |
| "Can you prove this corridor should be built?" | ROUTE can structure the service case and evidence holds, but construction readiness requires engineering, environmental, right-of-way, funding, and agency gates. |
| "Can this replace our GIS or model?" | No. ROUTE should use those systems as evidence and identity surfaces. |
| "Can we call this a guaranteed SLA?" | No. ROUTE can create planning promises and legal-SLA holds until operating evidence and authority review close. |
| "Can we say a state or operator validated this?" | No. Validation, endorsement, and approval require explicit external review records. |

## Pass Gate

A first-client prospect passes only if it can support all five conditions:

1. Named network or corridor scope.
2. Named service pain.
3. Existing evidence surfaces.
4. Budget owner, sponsor, or paid pilot route.
5. Acceptance of claim boundaries.

If any condition fails, keep the relationship warm but do not treat it as a
first-client candidate.

## Boundary

This package is a sales-readiness and client-selection brief. It does not claim
paying customers, qualified leads, procurement readiness, agency endorsement,
operator endorsement, market demand, legal-SLA readiness, traffic-demand proof,
numeric ROI, construction readiness, funding eligibility, public-readiness, or
external validation.
