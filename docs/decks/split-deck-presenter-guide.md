# Split Deck Presenter Guide

## Use The Decks In This Order

| Moment | Deck | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Political, funder, state, or public meeting | `interstate-2-0-pitch.pptx` | Sell the Interstate 2.0 vision without naming ROUTE. |
| Technical sponsor, analyst, or implementation follow-up | `route-technology-story.pptx` | Explain how ROUTE makes the vision inspectable, refinable, and evidence-bounded. |

Do not open with ROUTE unless the audience already asked about the technology.
The first deck is the vision. The second deck is the machinery.

## Core Talk Track

Use this one-sentence spine:

> Interstate 2.0 gives roads the service hierarchy rail already has, and ROUTE is
> the evidence engine that lets states, industry, communities, and funders refine
> that vision before major commitments are made.

For public audiences, stop before "and ROUTE..." unless asked how the plan is
made inspectable.

## Standard Map Caption Pattern

Use this caption shape for every map shown in a meeting, deck appendix, or
rehearsal packet:

```text
Map level: structural / heuristic / held-claim / evidence-valid.
Claim label: <label>.
Excluded claims: SLA readiness, upgrade readiness, terminal proof,
asset-condition proof, environmental readiness, construction readiness,
official adoption, numeric ROI.
Evidence pointer: <artifact path or gate>.
```

Do not show a map without saying whether it is a structural surface, heuristic
surface, held-claim surface, or evidence-valid claim.

## Deck A: Interstate 2.0 Pitch

| Slide | Talk Track | Do Not Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Promise, not a map | "The country does not just need another highway map. It needs a national service promise." | This is the official plan. |
| 2. Built for another era | "The old system was transformational, but freight, energy, labor, resilience, and automation now need an operating system." | The old interstate system failed. |
| 3. Problem leaders recognize | "Gridlock, fragmented resilience, inconsistent freight lanes, and missing nodes are already visible to leaders." | We have proven every bottleneck or cost. |
| 4. Service promise ladder | "T1/T2/T3/T4 give roads differentiated jobs: spine, regional connector, feeder, terminal access." | These windows are guaranteed operating commitments. |
| 5. Roads need rail hierarchy | "Rail and metro systems already use hierarchy. Roads need that same logic so priorities stop blurring together." | All interstates should be treated the same. |
| 6. T1 reliability | "T1 is where the country buys reliability through roadway needs, managed lanes, interchange fixes, resilience, and alternatives." | These exact improvements are construction-ready. |
| 7. Promise visible | "The map is a vision surface: it makes the operating idea legible." | The map proves SLA, upgrade, asset, or terminal readiness. |
| 8. Every tier has a job | "The point is not one national spine only. The system needs spine, regional, access, terminal, relay, and resilience layers." | Lower tiers are leftovers. |
| 9. Relay hubs | "Relay hubs are the aviation model for freight: crew bases, charging, handoffs, maintenance, and future automation." | Relay hubs already solve labor, EV, or AV adoption. |
| 10. Win-win | "States, industry, funders, drivers, communities, and innovation each get a place in the story." | Everyone already agrees. |
| 11. What people should feel | "This is not only freight throughput. Farm regions, small metros, drivers, emergency access, communities, and funders all need to see themselves." | Freight ROI is the only value. |
| 12. Who is at the table | "The plan should get requirements before concrete: state delivery, freight operations, rural access, community health, environment, and funding gates." | Stakeholder concerns are already resolved. |
| 13. Safe to say yes | "The safe ask is not a finished build map. It is standards, intake, evidence, and pilots." | Fund construction now because the map is done. |
| 14. Fundable sequence | "Fund story, evidence, and pilots. Move to major commitments only when evidence, owners, and review close." | We have a numeric ROI or final cost. |
| 15. Closing | "Interstate 2.0 is a national service network people can fund, refine, and believe in." | The plan is final. |

## Deck A Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Is this a federal takeover of state planning?" | "No. It is a national service framework that helps states show why their priorities matter nationally." |
| "Are these delivery times guaranteed?" | "No. They are planning targets for comparing service roles until reliability evidence closes." |
| "Where is the ROI?" | "The near-term ask is the ROI evidence contract: price year, sources, benefits, costs, uncertainty, exclusions, and review." |
| "Is the map final?" | "No. It is a schematic vision surface. Detailed proof belongs in the evidence package." |
| "What do communities get?" | "Concerns enter before concrete: noise, safety, displacement, air, runoff, habitat, access, and mitigation." |

## Transition From Deck A To Deck B

Use this bridge:

> If the vision is interesting, the next question is: how do we keep it honest
> and refinable? That is where ROUTE comes in.

Then open the ROUTE technology deck.

## Deck B: ROUTE Technology Story

| Slide | Talk Track | Do Not Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. ROUTE makes it refinable | "The first deck said what the country should build toward. ROUTE shows how to test, revise, and defend it." | ROUTE is the public brand for every audience. |
| 2. Not a prettier map | "ROUTE is a research system, Rust toolkit, generated map pipeline, VTRACE evidence package, and review process." | ROUTE is only a map renderer. |
| 3. Job ROUTE does | "A public goal becomes a requirement, model change, plan refinement, and staged option." | ROUTE automatically knows the best answer. |
| 4. Demo loop | "State, industry, and community requirements can change the service promise, option, hold, or evidence need." | Stakeholder input is just comments. |
| 5. Stress rhythm example | "A 250-mile gate passes; a tighter 225-mile stress exposes named candidates and held midpoint gaps." | The 225-mile stress proves those stops should be built. |
| 6. Promises into artifacts | "T1/T2/T3/T4 promises become artifact surfaces, but the windows stay planning targets until evidence closes." | These are guaranteed service levels. |
| 7. Generated maps | "Maps are generated contract surfaces. At deck scale they are previews; details live in artifacts." | The screenshot is proof. |
| 8. Bundle-first identity | "ROUTE keeps stable service identity separate from mutable route labels and map ids." | Route labels alone are stable claim identity. |
| 9. Recursive optimizer | "The optimizer works down tiers and bubbles infeasible lower-tier pressure back upward." | It is one magic score. |
| 10. Evidence labels | "Implemented, heuristic, and held labels let us sell vision without pretending proof is closed." | Everything is proven. |
| 11. ROI framework | "ROI starts as a framework: value stack, cost stack, and a gate before numbers." | We have benefit-cost ratios today. |
| 12. Review system | "The review system is part of the product: parliament, stakeholders, editorial gates, VTRACE." | Dissent is a problem to hide. |
| 13. Claim package | "A claim package contains the artifact, evidence posture, and role tension." | A strong pitch is enough. |
| 14. What to fund next | "Fund the story package, decision package, and demo package so a requirement can visibly change the plan." | Fund a final construction program now. |
| 15. Closing | "Interstate 2.0 is the vision. ROUTE is how it becomes inspectable, challengeable, refinable, and fundable." | ROUTE replaces state, engineering, or public review. |

## Deck B Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Is this implemented?" | "Parts are implemented and validated as artifacts and commands; claims remain labeled as implemented, heuristic, source-needed, or held." |
| "What exactly changes when a requirement arrives?" | "The current demo shows requirements becoming thresholds, diagnostics, candidate dockets, promotion scaffolds, or evidence holds." |
| "Why VTRACE?" | "VTRACE keeps mission, requirements, specs, evidence, verification, validation, and review connected so the pitch cannot outrun proof." |
| "Can this produce ROI?" | "It can produce the evidence contract for ROI now; numeric ROI requires sources, price year, scope, uncertainty, exclusions, and review." |
| "Why not show every technical detail?" | "This deck sells the refinement engine. Detailed artifacts belong in the demo runbook and evidence package." |

## Audience-Specific Emphasis

| Audience | Lead With | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Politicians | National service promise, jobs, resilience, regional fairness, visible staged ask. | Technical architecture, package names, proof minutiae. |
| Funders | Safe sequence: story, intake, evidence, pilots, ROI source pack. | Premature construction or ROI claims. |
| State DOTs | State authority, delivery checklist, phasing, maintenance, right-of-way, agency role. | "Washington hands down a map." |
| Freight / industry | Service windows, bottlenecks, terminal access, relay hubs, charging, parking, HOS, data ask. | Guaranteed operating outcomes. |
| Communities | Concerns before concrete: noise, air, safety, displacement, access, runoff, habitat. | Net-benefit language that erases local harm. |
| Technical reviewers | Artifact generation, bundle identity, evidence labels, VTRACE, demo runbook. | Pure vision without proof posture. |

## Round 1: Local / Regional Meeting Controls

Use with MPO/RPO, district, local official, freight advisory, rural, or community
meetings.

| Item | Required Action |
|---|---|
| Opening posture | "This is requirements before concrete. The map is a structural service surface, not a project list." |
| Intake artifact | Use `docs/how-to/local-regional-intake-template.md`. |
| Rural example card | "Harvest access, trauma-center access, evacuation, and farm-to-terminal movement become T3/T4 obligations or held evidence rows." |
| Closeout | Requirements captured, dissent preserved, map posture stated, next evidence/demo ask selected. |
| Allowed asks | Intake package, evidence package, demo fixture, source pack, or bounded pilot. |
| Do not ask for | Endorsement, construction funding, project approval, or map adoption. |

## Round 2: State Review Controls

Use with state DOT, governor-sponsor, state freight office, and legislative
transportation staff.

| State Input | ROUTE Artifact Target | Evidence Label | Held Claim | AASHTO Peer Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery constraint | `docs/how-to/round2-state-intake-payload.md` | source-needed / held | Funding, ROW, maintenance, environmental, lifecycle readiness. | Which constraints are shared or cross-border? |
| Freight data ask | ROI/source-pack or demo artifact | source-needed / heuristic | Reliability, PTI, terminal, parking, HOS, weight/clearance claim. | Which OD lanes or terminals matter regionally? |
| Rural/access ask | T3/T4 access row or source pack | source-needed / held | Access coverage or route promotion. | Which access obligations cross state markets? |
| Community/environmental concern | Dissent / hold row | held | Mitigation, equity, or impact resolution. | Which burdens are regional or repeated? |

Use the governor-safe line from `docs/how-to/round2-state-intake-payload.md`.

## Round 3: AASHTO Regional Association Controls

Safe opening:

> This is shared service evidence, not state wins. The goal is to expose which
> regional claims need sources, sensitivity, dissent, and owner review before
> any option is promoted.

| Objection | Safe Answer |
|---|---|
| "Why does your state get the spine?" | "T1/T2/T3/T4 are service roles. Selection claims require regional evidence, sensitivity, and holds." |
| "This map demotes our connector." | "The map is structural. Treatment can change only through contact, access, promise, or evidence rows." |
| "Who governs cross-border promises?" | "ROUTE does not create authority, governance, eligibility, or cross-border commitments." |
| "Where is ROI?" | "ROI remains a source-pack gate: price year, benefits, costs, uncertainty, exclusions, negative cases, and review." |
| "Where are rural and environmental burdens?" | "They are required fields in the regional packet, not afterthoughts." |

Required close: regional evidence/source-pack standard or bounded pilot. Do not
close on construction or endorsement.

## Congressional Hearing Controls

Use this module before any elected-official hearing, member briefing, or staff
roundtable.

| Objection | Safe Answer |
|---|---|
| "Are you asking Congress to fund a new interstate buildout?" | "No. The ask is standards, intake, evidence, demo/source packs, and bounded pilots before major commitments." |
| "What is the ROI?" | "No numeric ROI is promoted. Source pack first: price year, benefit/cost classes, uncertainty, exclusions, and negative or marginal cases." |
| "Why trust the map?" | "Do not trust a map alone. Maps are structural or held-claim surfaces until claim-specific evidence closes." |
| "How do communities avoid repeated highway harms?" | "Displacement, air, noise, runoff, habitat, safety, access, mitigation, and who bears costs enter before feature promotion." |

Permitted asks: story, intake, evidence, demo, source pack, standards, or bounded
pilot package.

Forbidden phrases: official plan, construction authorization, guaranteed
service, positive ROI, agency endorsement, statutory compliance, NEPA complete,
design compliant, safety compliant.

## What This Does Not Authorize

The communications package does not authorize, endorse, or prove:

- official federal, state, regional, or local plan adoption;
- construction, project approval, or funding obligation;
- guaranteed service windows or operating SLAs;
- numeric ROI, benefit-cost ratio, or federal exposure;
- statutory or program eligibility;
- NEPA, public-involvement, safety, civil-rights, or design-standard compliance;
- state, regional, congressional, FHWA, USDOT, stakeholder, or community
  endorsement.

## Red Lines

Do not say:

- "official plan";
- "construction-ready";
- "guaranteed service windows";
- "positive ROI";
- "stakeholders validated this";
- "the map proves readiness";
- "relay hubs solve labor, EV, or autonomy";
- "ROUTE replaces state DOTs or public review."

## Close Every Meeting With One Ask

Pick one:

| Ask | Use When |
|---|---|
| Fund the story package | Audience is vision-aligned but needs shared language. |
| Fund the intake package | Audience has state, industry, or community requirements to add. |
| Fund the evidence package | Audience wants proof before endorsing. |
| Fund the demo package | Audience wants to see the requirement-to-refinement loop. |
| Fund a pilot package | Audience is ready to study a hub, access, charging, resilience, or interchange package. |

Do not leave with a vague "let's keep talking." Leave with the next evidence or
pilot gate.
