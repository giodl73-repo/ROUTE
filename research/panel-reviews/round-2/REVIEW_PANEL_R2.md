## Remaining PP Items

### PP1 — Blocking (1 item)

**PP1.1 — A.2 independence test: confidence intervals for ρ=0.81 STRAHNET correlation** *(Neumark, Adamic)*

The v1.4 dimension expansion increases the stakes of the external validation test. The ρ=0.81 Pearson correlation between ROUTE rubric scores and STRAHNET classification is the primary external validity evidence for the calibrated instrument. Currently reported as a point estimate without confidence interval. With n=227 corridors, the 95% CI for ρ=0.81 is approximately [0.76, 0.85] — calculable via Fisher z-transformation. Required: (a) add CI to A.2 Section 4; (b) report the correlation separately for the pre-v1.4 (12-dimension) and post-v1.4 (16-dimension) rubric versions to confirm that the dimension expansion did not degrade predictive alignment with STRAHNET.

*Blocking because: the external validity of the calibrated rubric underpins every downstream paper that cites dimension scores. Without CI, the ρ=0.81 claim is a point estimate with unknown precision at a dimension count that increased by 33% since the correlation was first computed.*

### PP2 — Important (3 items)

**PP2.1 — C.4 algorithmic scalability: Hungarian O(n³) at national scale** *(Adamic)*

The bipartite load-matching model in C.4 uses the Hungarian algorithm. At a national relay hub processing 2,000+ trucks/day with a 400-mile catchment containing potentially 10,000+ available loads, O(n³) complexity becomes computationally infeasible within the 5–10 minute matching window. Recommended replacement: auction algorithm (Bertsekas 1988, O(n²) in practice) or greedy successive shortest path. Add to C.4 Section 5: algorithm complexity analysis, benchmark against hub throughput requirements, and recommended replacement algorithm with expected runtime at peak load. The load-matching finding and economic valuation are unaffected by algorithm choice; the paper needs to demonstrate that the proposed mechanism is computationally tractable at scale.

**PP2.2 — B.5 K_port empirical calibration: gate count citations beyond POLA/POLB** *(Puentes, McKinnon)*

B.5's port connector standard uses peak-hour demand based on drayage patterns. Gate count data for LA/Long Beach is cited from POLA/POLB Gate Statistics 2023, but equivalent data for Savannah, NY/NJ, and Seattle/Tacoma is not yet cited. POLA/POLB is a non-representative case (largest port, highest throughput). Add empirical calibration for at least 3 additional ports using GPA Annual Report 2023, PANYNJ freight reports, and NWSA annual statistics before generalizing the K_port standard.

**PP2.3 — F.3 + C.4 decarbonization compound effects: quantify the relay+AV+electrification stack** *(McKinnon)*

C.4 states 45M fewer empty truck-miles/day nationally from load matching; F.3's AV section claims 20–35% fuel savings from platooning on managed lanes. These claims are not compounded with E.2's EV component ($8B, DCFC ≤ 50 mi spacing). Add to F.3 Section 7 (or E.2 Appendix A): a stacked emissions reduction model showing (a) empty-mile baseline reduction from C.4, (b) platooning fuel savings on managed lanes, (c) EV adoption curve on DCFC infrastructure, (d) combined reduction in trucking sector GHG by 2035 and 2050. Without compounding, the module understates the climate case for I2.0.
