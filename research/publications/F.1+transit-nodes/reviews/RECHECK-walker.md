### PP1.2 — Proximity vs. Operational Access (12.4M figure)

The original concern: the 12.4 million figure was derived from a 30-mile geographic radius and applied throughout the paper as if it represented transit-accessible population. For a transit-dependent traveler — by definition someone without a car — living within 30 miles of a controlled-access freight hub does not constitute access. The figure conflated physical proximity with operational accessibility.

The revision adds a "Proximity vs. Operational Access" paragraph immediately after the hub table in Section 3. The paragraph makes the required distinction explicitly: it states that the 12.4 million figure measures geographic proximity, not operational access; that zero-vehicle households cannot reach a hub on a controlled-access corridor without a local feeder connection; and that the finding is properly framed as "would benefit from a transit connection if one were provided." The paragraph also acknowledges the feeder cost gap ($500–800 million nationally for a baseline van/shuttle network) and scopes the 14× efficiency finding to the hub increment only, excluding feeder costs.

This is precisely the reframing the original review required. The paper no longer asserts that 12.4 million people have access; it asserts that 12.4 million people live near a location where access could be provided. The distinction is analytically correct and scientifically honest. The feeder cost acknowledgment is a meaningful addition — it prevents readers from treating the hub increment cost as the all-in cost of serving these communities.

The concern is substantively resolved.

**P3 note (future work):** F.1 should cite ACS table B08101 (means of transportation to work) for hub-area communities to estimate what fraction of the 12.4 million currently have access to any transit mode — not intercity transit, but any transit. This would allow a more precise estimate of the operationally accessible fraction: communities with existing local transit are closer to hub access than communities with none. A sensitivity table (low-transit-access communities vs. communities with existing FTA 5311 service) would sharpen the policy recommendation considerably.
