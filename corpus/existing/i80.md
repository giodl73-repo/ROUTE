## Overview

I-80 is a transcontinental Interstate from the San Francisco Bay Area to
Teaneck, New Jersey, crossing eleven states and linking western ports and
metropolitan regions with the central plains, the Chicago freight complex, and
the New York region. As an Interstate it is part of the National Highway System,
the Strategic Highway Network, and the National Highway Freight Network. Those
federal classifications establish national mobility, defense, and freight
importance; they do not by themselves prove that every segment needs the same
upgrade or that any proposed treatment has positive ROI.

## Notable Segments

- The Bay Area and Sacramento approaches, the Chicago/Joliet region, and the New Jersey terminus are high-complexity urban interfaces that should be reviewed separately from the corridor-wide unweighted HPMS segment mean.
- Donner Pass is the flagship winter-resilience evidence target. ROUTE has an executable closure scenario, but observed closure frequency, truck-suitable alternate capacity, and recovery performance remain held.
- The Wyoming and central-plains segments make long-distance continuity and weather exposure visible, while the current record still needs state and segment distributions before it can support a uniform corridor traffic claim.
- The I-35/I-80 Des Moines junction has a repeatable Iowa 511 evidence path, but the current observation window remains snapshot-limited and cannot support annual failure or recovery claims.

## Interstate 2.0 Fit

- Treat I-80 as a portfolio of segment-specific problems rather than one uniform construction program.
- Parliament held and narrowed the Des Moines independent-transfer-path hypothesis into a validation plan; no design or capital treatment is selected.
- Require observed reliability or closure evidence before promoting timed-freight SLA claims.
- Keep route labels, map visibility, and federal network membership separate from investment readiness.

## Flagship Claim Holds

- A4=9.0 remains a heuristic designation score; I-80 has no direct international border terminus and no route-level border-flow calculation is attached.
- B4=8.5 remains heuristic until STRAHNET and installation proximity are represented by source-level rows rather than a single designation note.
- C4=9.0 supports an agricultural-production-access hypothesis, not a proven long-haul export-mode share.
- The 11,344 AADT and 24% truck values are unweighted means across matched HPMS segments, not uniform corridor-wide conditions.
- A3 is a BPR-derived reliability proxy rather than observed NPMRDS PTI.
- No official-plan, construction-readiness, guaranteed-SLA, positive-ROI, or agency-endorsement claim is made.

## Open Questions

- What share of I-80 mileage and how many route segments are represented in the HPMS, NBI, Census, FEMA, FARS, intermodal, and charger joins?
- How do AADT, truck share, reliability, safety, bridge condition, and hazard exposure vary by state and operating segment?
- Should A4 be removed or replaced with an NHFN/PHFS freight-role dimension for corridors without direct border service?
- Can B4 and C4 be rebuilt from source-custody rows with installation distance, commodity flow, terminal, year, and calculation fields?
- Will comparable topology, calibrated demand, and community-bounded alternatives confirm or falsify the Des Moines independent-transfer-path hypothesis?
