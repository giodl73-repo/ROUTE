# Environmental Community

## Who this is

Environmental advocacy organizations (Sierra Club, Nature Conservancy, local watershed groups), environmental justice advocates, and public health researchers who study the impacts of highway infrastructure on ecosystems and communities. Brings both ecological and health perspectives.

## Primary concerns

**Air quality and health near corridors**: Diesel emissions from freight traffic create elevated PM2.5 and NOx concentrations within 300–500 meters of major freight corridors. Communities living adjacent to high-freight interstates have documented higher rates of asthma, cardiovascular disease, and premature mortality. This is a public health issue with a geographic dimension — low-income communities are disproportionately located near high-freight corridors.

**Stormwater runoff**: Highway runoff carries petroleum, heavy metals, and de-icing chemicals into adjacent waterways. Impervious surface area scales with highway lane-miles. New corridors generate new runoff; existing corridors with aging drainage infrastructure have inadequate treatment.

**Habitat fragmentation**: Highways act as barriers to wildlife movement. Species that can't cross a highway effectively have their habitat bisected. Underpasses and wildlife crossings mitigate this; they're rarely included in standard highway design and almost never retrofitted.

**Noise**: Highway noise affects quality of life, property values, and human health (sleep disruption, cardiovascular stress) in adjacent communities. Noise barriers are a standard mitigation but are distributed inequitably — higher-income communities adjacent to highways are more likely to have noise walls than lower-income communities.

**EV transition opportunity**: The transition from diesel freight to electric freight reduces corridor air quality impacts dramatically. Interstate 2.0's EV charging infrastructure accelerates this transition. The environmental community supports this feature as an emissions reduction mechanism.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Population within 300m of corridor (health impact zone)
- Adjacent sensitive land uses (wetlands, wildlife habitat, environmental justice communities)
- Existing noise barrier coverage vs. community need
- Stormwater management infrastructure age and adequacy
- Wildlife crossing presence on significant habitat corridors

## Lens for scoring

The environmental community grounds D1 (Climate Resilience) in ecosystem vulnerability in addition to infrastructure vulnerability. A corridor through a major wetland system or wildlife corridor has a different resilience profile than the infrastructure-only view captures. D2 (Multimodal Integration) scores higher for the environmental community when the multimodal integration reduces vehicle emissions, not just vehicle counts.
