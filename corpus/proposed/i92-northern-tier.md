# I-92 — Northern Tier Transcontinental

## Overview

The United States has no interstate highway across its northern border belt. The northernmost existing transcontinental is I-90 (Boston-Seattle), which runs 200-400 miles south of the Canadian border through most of its length. The entire swath of Montana, North Dakota, Minnesota's Iron Range, the Upper Peninsula of Michigan, and northern Maine has no interstate connection.

US-2 runs the full 2,571-mile alignment from Houlton ME to Everett WA — the only road that does. An upgrade to interstate standard would become **I-92**, the highest-numbered new transcontinental, serving the northern border from coast to coast.

**Why I-92**: Even numbers increase north to south for E-W interstates. I-90 is the northern existing transcontinental. I-92 would be the next number for a new transcontinental further north (I-94 exists Chicago-Billings; I-96 exists Michigan only).

## B1 Score = 10.0

US-2 scores maximum B1 (redundancy need) because there is literally no parallel interstate within 300-500 miles along most of its route. I-90 is the nearest alternative for most of Montana and North Dakota — that's 300 miles south. This is the strongest B1 = 10.0 in the entire corpus, confirming the Northern Tier gap is the most structurally isolated zone in the national network.

## Strategic Case

1. **ICBM command corridors**: The two active ICBM bases (Malmstrom AFB MT and Minot AFB ND) are located along the US-2 corridor. The Air Force's nuclear force projection depends on road access that currently runs on two-lane US highway. An I-92 upgrade would provide all-weather, high-capacity military logistics access to the nation's strategic deterrent.

2. **Red River Valley agricultural export**: The Red River Valley (eastern ND/western MN) produces more spring wheat per acre than anywhere in the US. Current grain export routing goes south on I-29 to Gulf ports. I-92 would open direct westward routing to Pacific Northwest ports (Duluth/Superior already has Great Lakes export; Tacoma/Portland are the Pacific outlet) — cutting export distance for Montana/ND grain by 400+ miles.

3. **Northern border gateway**: US-2 crosses the Canadian border near Havre MT and intersects with Canadian provincial roads. As USMCA expands trade with Canada's Prairie Provinces (Saskatchewan, Manitoba grain; Alberta oil), a northern tier US interstate improves the infrastructure basis for this trade.

4. **Coverage gap closure**: The 44 Montana gap counties and 44 North Dakota gap counties (from B.1 analysis) are primarily served by US-2. This single upgrade closes the largest contiguous geographic coverage gap in the continental US (I-90 to Canada border, 300-500 miles).

5. **I-92/I-31 T1/T1 junction**: Where I-92 crosses the proposed I-31 (near Havre MT or Shelby MT), this would be the northernmost T1/T1 junction in the continental US — currently, there is no T1 within 300 miles of the Montana/Canadian border in any direction.
