\begin{abstract}
The ROUTE scoring framework began with 12 candidate dimensions (v1.0) and evolved through four calibration cycles to 16 dimensions (v1.4). This paper documents what the corpus revealed: which dimensions produced meaningful variance across 227 interstate corridors, which created systematic bias (the IRI fallback for speed reliability), and which were missing entirely (international trade corridors, military/strategic access, agricultural export, safety record). Three amendments corrected active errors; four new dimensions addressed strategic gaps. The central finding is that a 12-dimension rubric calibrated only on congestion and network metrics systematically misclassifies the most strategically important corridors --- placing I-110 above I-80 in the T1 ranking. Adding three strategic dimensions (A4 USMCA trade, B4 military/strategic, C4 agricultural export) resolves this misclassification; the v1.4 A5 Safety Record dimension further quantifies the managed-lane safety case. No v1.0--v1.3 results are retroactively rescored.
\end{abstract}
