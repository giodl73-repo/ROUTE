# State DOT Planner

## Who this is

A transportation planner or program manager at a state Department of Transportation. Responsible for the long-range transportation plan, federal funding applications (RAISE, INFRA, NHPP, BUILD), project delivery, and coordination with FHWA. Lives at the intersection of what's needed and what's fundable.

## Primary concerns

**Federal funding match requirements**: Interstate improvements require federal-state cost sharing. A corridor that scores well on national metrics but requires a state funding match the state can't provide is a corridor that doesn't get built regardless of its merits. ROUTE's proposals need to be realistic about funding structure.

**Maintenance burden**: Every new lane-mile the state adds to the interstate system is a lane-mile it has to maintain forever. States with growing backlogs (nearly all of them) are cautious about adding to the inventory. Proposed corridors need to account for lifecycle costs, not just construction costs.

**Right-of-way**: Right-of-way acquisition is often the longest and most expensive part of a new corridor. Corridors that follow existing rail corridors, utility easements, or existing US highway alignments have lower ROW acquisition costs and faster delivery timelines.

**Political feasibility**: Proposed corridors that cross multiple congressional districts require broad political support. A corridor that is economically justified but concentrated in one party's territory may not survive the appropriations process. State DOTs are pragmatic about this.

**Federal designation process**: Getting a corridor into the Interstate Highway System requires FHWA approval, which requires meeting design standards, traffic volume thresholds, and often an environmental review process that can take 10+ years.

## What ROUTE should capture from this stakeholder

- Federal funding eligibility for proposed corridor features
- Estimated right-of-way acquisition complexity (existing alignment vs. greenfield)
- State DOT long-range transportation plan references for proposed corridors
- Timeline estimate from concept to construction given current federal processes

## Lens for scoring

The state DOT planner grounds proposals in delivery reality. A corridor that scores well on all dimensions but has no plausible funding path or a 20-year permitting timeline is a corridor that exists on paper. ROUTE's design proposals should acknowledge this dimension.
