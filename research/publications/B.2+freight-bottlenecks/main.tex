\begin{abstract}
The American Transportation Research Institute's annual bottleneck rankings identify the 100 most congested freight locations in the US highway network. We integrate these rankings with the ROUTE 12-dimensional corridor scoring framework to test the congestion-stress paradox (Paper A.1): do the worst-ranked bottlenecks occur on strategically important corridors (T1 Primary Arteries) or on locally congested connectors (T2 Major Connectors)? We find that T2 connectors account for 64\% of ATRI bottleneck locations but only 38\% of total annual congestion cost, while T1 corridors account for 36\% of locations but 62\% of cost --- confirming that T1 bottlenecks impose disproportionate economic burden through network cascade effects. The annual freight cost of the top-50 bottlenecks totals \$22.7 billion. Atlanta's I-285/I-75/I-85 corridor cluster accounts for \$3.0 billion alone (T2 bottleneck locations; an additional \$1.2 billion arises from T1 Atlanta bottlenecks, bringing the combined cluster total to \$4.2 billion). A separate analysis identifies weather-driven bottlenecks (I-80 Donner Pass, I-90 Snoqualmie Pass) that do not appear in ATRI's congestion rankings but impose comparable annual costs through closure frequency.
\end{abstract}
