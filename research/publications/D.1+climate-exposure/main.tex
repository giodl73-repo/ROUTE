\begin{abstract}
We compute climate exposure for all 227 interstate corridors in the ROUTE v1.2 corpus using
three primary data sources: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) for flood zone overlay,
NOAA 2050 sea level rise projections for coastal corridors, and USDA/FEMA historical closure
frequency data for winter precipitation and wildfire corridors. The D1 dimension of the ROUTE
rubric --- composite climate vulnerability scored 0--10 --- combines consecutive SFHA miles
(70\% weight) and total SFHA miles (30\% weight) for each corridor. The five highest-scoring
D1 corridors are I-10 Gulf Coast Louisiana (8.4), I-10 Gulf Coast Texas (7.8), I-80 Donner
Pass (7.8), I-90 Snoqualmie (7.1), and I-35 Oklahoma (7.2). Under NOAA intermediate sea level
rise projections (0.5m by 2050), the Gulf Coast I-10 D1 score rises from 8.4 to a projected
9.1, making it the most climate-exposed corridor in the national system by mid-century. The
2050 exposure profile fundamentally changes the investment priority order: Gulf Coast I-10
hardening becomes the most urgent climate adaptation investment in the national highway system.
\end{abstract}
