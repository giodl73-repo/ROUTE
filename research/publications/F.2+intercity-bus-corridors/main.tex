\title{Intercity Bus Corridors: Travel Time, Coverage, and Equity on the Interstate 2.0 Network}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}
\date{May 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Managed freight lanes on Tier 1 Primary Arteries create the physical conditions for intercity bus service that does not currently exist outside the Northeast Corridor: scheduled, reliable, and affordable intercity transit at 62 mph average speed with Planning Time Index $\leq$ 1.15. We analyze 12 T1 bus corridors enabled by the Interstate 2.0 hub network (F.1) and find that I2.0 bus travel times are 28--45\% faster than current bus alternatives on every corridor and competitive with Amtrak where Amtrak service exists. On corridors where no intercity bus or rail service currently exists --- including Houston--Chicago, Atlanta--Dallas, and the full northern transcontinental --- I2.0 bus creates entirely new intercity connectivity. At a targeted fare of \$0.12/mile, T1 express bus is the lowest-cost intercity travel option in its markets. The 12 T1 corridors together would serve an estimated 24 million annual passengers at market equilibrium, with disproportionate benefit to zero-vehicle households and rural communities within 30 miles of T1 hub stops.
\end{abstract}
