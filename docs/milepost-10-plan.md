# Milepost 10 Plan — Recursive Tier Optimizer

## Goal

Milepost 10 turns T1/T2/T3/T4 selection from hand-shaped map work into a
repeatable recursive optimizer.

The goal is to make every line, stop, region, and Beck bend traceable to:

```text
SLA promise -> graph choice -> METIS split objective -> selected route/stop column -> validation gate
```

The optimizer must support both graph views:

- **Primal stop graph**: stops/cities/interchanges are vertices; route segments are edges.
- **Dual route graph**: routes/service columns are vertices; shared stops, transfers, overlaps, and relief relationships are edges.

METIS is the splitter. ROUTE decides what the split balances: stops, miles,
freight, population/market access, resilience, or a hybrid service objective.

## Done Criteria

Milepost 10 is done when:

- T1 line and stop selection are generated by optimizer artifacts, not embedded Beck choices.
- T2 is solved as full regional treatments inside T1-bounded regions.
- T3/T4 access pressure can roll back upward as explicit repair or upgrade witnesses.
- Every METIS split records graph kind, objective, weights, selected regions, and validation status.
- The Beck map reads from selected optimizer outputs or has a gate proving it matches them.
- `cargo test -p route-network`, route optimizer gates, and map diagnostics pass.

## Non-Goals

- Do not claim the first optimizer is globally optimal.
- Do not use Beck geometry to invent connectivity.
- Do not hand-pick route/stop outcomes outside the generated artifacts.
- Do not promote source-gated or incomplete corridors without exception rows.

The first optimizer can be deterministic and modest. It only needs to make the
choice process explicit, replayable, and hard to fudge.
