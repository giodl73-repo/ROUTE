\title{The Economics of Corridor Closures:\\
       Freight Cost and Redundancy Value}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}
\date{May 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Corridor closures impose freight costs that are highly non-linear with closure duration and
poorly correlated with V/C ratio alone. We compute the freight disruption cost for the 15
highest-risk corridors in the ROUTE corpus using a model combining closure frequency (from
historical data), closure duration distribution, freight volume at closure point, and rerouting
penalty per truck-hour. The five highest-cost corridors account for \$5.1 billion in annual
freight disruption cost ($5.1 billion after demand suppression correction; see Section~4): Donner Pass (\$2.4B), Gulf Coast I-10 (\$1.2B), Dallas Interchange
(\$0.8B), I-95 Baltimore (\$0.6B), and I-35 Oklahoma (\$0.4B). The redundancy value ---
computed as the cost difference between a closure with and without an adequate alternate ---
reveals that the most expensive corridors to close are not always the most congested: Donner
Pass, with moderate V/C at the pass itself, costs 4.2$\times$ more per closure than the Dallas
interchange, which has higher V/C but better rerouting options. This finding supports a compound
exposure investment strategy \citep{ROUTE_B3} over single-dimension congestion relief.
\end{abstract}
