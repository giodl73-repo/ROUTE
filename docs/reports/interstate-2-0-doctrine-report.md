# Interstate 2.0 Doctrine Report

## Research Question

What doctrine should anchor the Interstate 2.0 communications package before
ROUTE promotes any corridor-specific, construction, SLA, or ROI claim?

## Decision This Supports

This report supports the public narrative decision for the ROUTE communications
package: use **Interstate 2.0 as a service-network doctrine** and keep ROUTE as
the evidence/refinement engine behind that doctrine.

The doctrine should be strong enough for politicians, funders, states, industry,
and communities, but bounded enough that it does not imply official adoption,
construction readiness, guaranteed service levels, or positive ROI.

## Executive Thesis

The United States already has a national road network. What it lacks is a
national **service hierarchy** for roads.

Interstate 2.0 says roads should be planned the way mature transport systems
are understood by users and funders: by role.

```text
T1 = national spine
T2 = regional connector
T3 = feeder / production-zone access
T4 = terminal / port / warehouse / last-mile access
```

That doctrine changes the planning question:

```text
not:  Which road deserves attention?
but:  What should the national network promise, and what evidence makes that
      promise credible?
```

## Findings

### ROUTE-01 — The central doctrine is service hierarchy.

**Source references:** `README.md` lines 6-13; `docs/research-conclusions.md`
lines 51-61.

**Observed constraint:** The current story distinguishes national spine,
regional connector, feeder/access, and terminal/local access instead of treating
all interstates as one category.

**Implication:** The public message should lead with "service promise" and
"service role," not with a road list or a software platform.

**Confidence:** High for communications doctrine; not a route-selection proof.

### ROUTE-02 — Promise windows are useful planning doctrine but not guarantees.

**Source references:** `docs/sla-promise-portfolio.md` lines 5-21 and 121-126;
`docs/reports/route-evidence-posture.md` lines 115-125.

**Observed constraint:** T1/T2/T3/T4 promise windows shape tier selection, but
the current portfolio remains design-grade and heuristic where direct
reliability validation is absent.

**Implication:** Decks and reports may use promise windows as planning targets.
They must not present them as guaranteed operating commitments.

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-03 — T1 is the national reliability spine, not simply the longest road.

**Source references:** `docs/sla-promise-portfolio.md` lines 37-52 and 92-108;
`docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 53-60 and 147-170.

**Observed constraint:** T1 selection is tied to 48h/36h promise pairs,
topology, coverage, relay/resilience obligation, and explicit exceptions, not
route fame or score alone.

**Implication:** The political story should say T1 is where the country buys
national reliability. Corridor-specific managed lane, interchange, and ROI
claims remain gated.

**Confidence:** Medium-high for doctrine; heuristic for investment claims.

### ROUTE-04 — T2 must be sold as regional treatment, not map decoration.

**Source references:** `docs/t2-regional-treatment.md` lines 3-24, 121-149,
and 151-178.

**Observed constraint:** T2 has contact, parent-trunk, duplicate-service,
demotion, and review rules. Review is the mechanism that keeps the map honest.

**Implication:** Interstate 2.0 should explain T2 as the regional engine inside
the national spine, not as a collection of thin lines drawn for coverage.

**Confidence:** High for doctrine; medium for any named T2 claim.

### ROUTE-05 — T3/T4 are what make the national system usable.

**Source references:** `docs/t3-t4-access-optimization.md` lines 3-21, 55-88,
and 112-123.

**Observed constraint:** T3/T4 own production, port, border, terminal,
warehouse, smaller metro, rural, and local freight access. They can reopen
higher tiers only through named proof, not score-only pressure.

**Implication:** The doctrine should not allow a freight-only or metro-only
story. Rural access, production zones, emergency access, ports, terminals, and
last-mile freight districts are first-class service obligations.

**Confidence:** High for doctrine; heuristic/gated for zone-specific claims.

### ROUTE-06 — Maps are persuasive, but maps are not proof.

**Source references:** `docs/map-publication-scope.md` lines 18-24 and 56-69;
`docs/reports/route-evidence-posture.md` lines 104-113.

**Observed constraint:** Current structural maps can make the system visible,
but render/publication validity does not prove SLA, transit, upgrade,
terminal-access, or asset-condition readiness.

**Implication:** Maps should remain central to the story, but every map slide
must preserve claim boundaries.

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-07 — ROI must be framed as an evidence contract before a number.

**Source references:** `docs/reports/route-roi-cost-framework.md` lines 25-56,
92-119, and 161-178.

**Observed constraint:** Current ROI/cost work defines benefit classes, cost
classes, price-year requirements, uncertainty, exclusions, negative-case
handling, and role review before any numeric ROI claim.

**Implication:** The funder ask should be "fund the evidence package that can
produce comparable options," not "fund this because ROI is proven."

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-08 — ROUTE's communications role is refinement, not authority.

**Source references:** `docs/vtrace/COMMUNICATIONS_STRATEGY.md` lines 14-28 and
63-73; `docs/reports/route-evidence-posture.md` lines 48-65.

**Observed constraint:** ROUTE is framed as an evidence-bounded argument system.
It can expose data, commands, confidence labels, stakeholder tension, and next
evidence steps, but it does not become an official plan or construction
authority.

**Implication:** The technology deck should follow the public doctrine deck,
not replace it. ROUTE is how the doctrine becomes inspectable and refinable.

**Confidence:** High.

## Doctrine Statement

Interstate 2.0 is the doctrine that roads should be planned as a national
service network:

1. **T1 buys national reliability.** It is the timed-freight spine and the place
   where managed lanes, interchange fixes, relay obligations, resilience, and
   alternate paths should be tested against national promises.
2. **T2 makes regions work.** It connects mega-regions, ports, borders,
   secondary metros, relief corridors, and regional service inside the T1 graph.
3. **T3 keeps access visible.** It protects production zones, farm regions,
   smaller metros, rural regions, and regional hubs from being erased by
   volume-only planning.
4. **T4 makes freight real.** It handles terminals, ports, gates, yards,
   warehouses, border approaches, local freight districts, truck parking,
   charging, staging, and last-mile obligations.
5. **Relay hubs make the doctrine human.** They connect service promises to
   drivers, regional jobs, charging, maintenance, handoffs, resilience, and
   future automation.
6. **Evidence labels protect trust.** A claim can be story-ready, implemented,
   heuristic, source-needed, gated, or held; the label travels with the claim.

## What This Doctrine Allows Us To Say Now

| Claim | Allowed Wording |
|---|---|
| Service hierarchy | "Interstate 2.0 gives roads differentiated service roles." |
| Promise ladder | "T1/T2/T3/T4 windows are planning targets for comparing service roles." |
| T1 | "T1 is where national reliability should be tested and bought." |
| T2 | "T2 is a regional treatment inside the T1 graph." |
| T3/T4 | "Access tiers keep production zones, rural regions, terminals, ports, and local freight districts visible." |
| Maps | "Maps make the doctrine visible as structural surfaces." |
| ROI | "ROI starts with an evidence contract before any number." |
| ROUTE | "ROUTE makes the doctrine inspectable, refinable, and evidence-bounded." |

## What This Doctrine Does Not Allow Us To Say Yet

- Interstate 2.0 is an official adopted plan.
- A named corridor, hub, interchange, or standard should be built.
- The service windows are guaranteed operating commitments.
- A map proves SLA, upgrade, terminal-access, transit, or asset-condition
  readiness.
- Any corridor, hub, or standard has positive ROI.
- Relay hubs solve labor, EV, AV, safety, or utilization problems.
- Stakeholders have validated the plan.

## Recommendations

### Adopt Now

| Recommendation | Owner | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Use service hierarchy as the flagship public doctrine. | communications owner / Scope Keeper | Deck and brief review for no official-plan or construction claims. |
| Keep the public deck solution-only and ROUTE-free. | communications owner | Search `interstate-2-0-pitch.md` for `route` / `ROUTE`. |
| Use promise windows as planning targets with disclaimers. | Citation Auditor / Scope Keeper | Review decks/briefs for "planning target" language. |
| Treat ROI as an evidence contract. | Freight Economist / Numeracy Checker | Require ROI/cost gate before any numeric claim. |

### Prototype Behind A Compatibility Boundary

| Prototype | Owner | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| A before/after requirement-to-refinement scenario fixture. | route-cli / research owner | Demo runbook plus command output showing artifact or evidence-label change. |
| T2 24h/12h promise-pair portfolio. | route-network / research owner | Generated artifact and role review before T2 public claims. |
| T3/T4 access obligation ledgers by zone. | route-network / stakeholder roles | Zone maps and access claims remain held until obligations are source-backed. |
| Relay hub operating model. | Freight Economist / Freight Industry | Labor, charging, dwell, utilization, and safety assumptions reviewed before promotion. |

### Reject Or Defer

| Claim / Action | Reason |
|---|---|
| Publishing a final national build map. | Current doctrine is story-ready, not official-plan or construction-ready. |
| Publishing numeric ROI. | Price year, source pack, uncertainty, exclusions, and review are not closed. |
| Promoting map screenshots as proof. | Map scope explicitly separates structural publication from SLA/upgrade/asset proof. |
| Claiming guaranteed service windows. | Direct reliability evidence and sensitivity ranges are still needed. |

## Non-Goals

- This report does not select corridors.
- This report does not define construction scope.
- This report does not estimate cost, ROI, benefit-cost ratio, or schedule.
- This report does not claim official endorsement.
- This report does not replace state DOT, environmental, engineering, safety, or
  community review.

## Gate

Decision: pass_with_risk

Rationale: The doctrine is ready for internal and draft communications use. It
is a strong organizing frame for the deck, briefs, and funder story. External
use remains gated for any named corridor, numeric, official-plan, construction,
guaranteed-SLA, or stakeholder-validation claim.
