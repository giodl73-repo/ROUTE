# Panel Synthesis — Round 1
## Freight Reliability on the NY–LA and Houston–Chicago Corridors: Capacity, Bottlenecks, and the Interstate 2.0 Case

---

## Headline Assessment

The paper identifies real and important freight reliability problems on two major national corridors, and the I-69 completion finding is the paper's strongest and most policy-actionable result. However, the PTI estimation methodology — BPR function applied at V/C 1.86 — is applied well beyond its calibrated range, undermining the quantitative reliability cost estimates on which the shipper SLA improvement claim depends. This is a blocking methodological gap that must be resolved by replacing the BPR-based PTI estimate with NPMRDS-based direct measurement. Secondary gaps include: incomplete shipper-level economic argument, O-D pair selection rationale, and I-69 implementation realism. The paper's qualitative corridor findings are solid; the quantitative claims need to be re-grounded.

---

## Earned Stakes

**E1 — Donner Pass and Bay Area V/C are the correct binding constraints for NY→LA.**
All reviewers accepted the binding constraint identification. Elefteriadou confirmed the geographic and capacity constraint decomposition is correct. Hanson added the directional imbalance nuance but did not dispute the constraint identification.

**E2 — The SLA framework (PTI × trip time = shipper planning buffer) is the right translation from traffic performance to freight economics.**
McKinnon, Neumark, and Puentes all accepted the SLA window concept. The 80-hour→48-hour compression is treated as a meaningful improvement by all reviewers, even those who question the PTI estimate underlying it.

**E3 — I-69 completion eliminates the HOU-CHI routing inefficiency and is the paper's strongest policy finding.**
All five reviewers found the I-69 section compelling. Puentes questioned the implementation timeline but accepted the operational analysis. The 290-mile shortening and elimination of Dallas/St. Louis interchange nodes is a concrete, geometry-grounded result that does not depend on the PTI model.

**E4 — The $8.2B annual reliability cost figure is plausible in order of magnitude.**
McKinnon provided a rough validation: Bay Area I-80 truck volumes at ATRI cost rates plausibly produce $600M–$900M in reliability cost for that segment alone, consistent with the NY-LA total. The figure needs transparent construction but is not implausible.

## P1 — Blocking Items

**P1.1 — Replace BPR-based PTI estimate with NPMRDS direct measurement.**
Elefteriadou identifies the BPR function as operating at V/C 1.86 — approximately 2 capacity-units above its calibrated range — making the PTI estimate unreliable at exactly the most congested and most important point in the analysis. The paper must replace the BPR-derived PTI with NPMRDS 5-minute interval travel time data for the Bay Area I-80 segment and Donner Pass segment. NPMRDS data is available through FHWA's Freight Performance Measures program. If NPMRDS data is unavailable for specific segments, the paper must use a queuing-theory model (D/D/1 or M/D/1) for oversaturated conditions, with the BPR estimate retained as a sensitivity comparison only.

---

## P2 — Important Items

**P2.1 — Transparent cost decomposition for $8.2B reliability figure.**
McKinnon requires: annual freight VMT by corridor segment, loaded truck-hours experiencing PTI > 1.0, ATRI cost rate applied ($/hour), and reliability premium multiplier. The $5.7B NY-LA and $2.5B HOU-CHI splits must be shown as arithmetic, not as black-box model outputs.

**P2.2 — Commodity-weighted reliability cost.**
McKinnon requires acknowledgment that the uniform reliability cost estimate overstates benefit for bulk freight and understates it for high-value JIT freight. Either a commodity-weighted estimate or an explicit caveat on the uniform treatment is required.

**P2.3 — Shipper-level economic worked example.**
Neumark requires at least one worked example at the carrier level: annual dollar benefit of PTI 1.86→1.15, net of managed lane tolls, for a carrier operating the NY-LA corridor 5 days per week. The paper must demonstrate that the I2.0 investment produces a net shipper-level benefit, not merely a corridor-level aggregate reliability improvement.

**P2.4 — Managed lane toll assumption and take-up rate.**
Neumark requires a stated managed lane toll assumption ($/mile or $/trip) and a discussion of freight carrier take-up rate. If toll costs offset a significant portion of the reliability gain for some freight segments, the effective coverage of the reliability benefit must be bounded.

**P2.5 — O-D pair selection rationale.**
Hanson requires a brief comparative analysis of candidate O-D pairs (minimum 4–6 alternatives) ranked by annual reliability cost or PTI, demonstrating that NY-LA and HOU-CHI are the highest-priority corridors for this analysis. Alternatively, the paper should explicitly reframe the study as "two illustrative cases" rather than a claim that these are the most important corridors.

**P2.6 — Directional flow analysis for NY→LA.**
Hanson requires acknowledgment of the westbound/eastbound imbalance on I-80 and a statement on whether the PTI calculation and reliability cost reflect directional flows or bidirectional averages.

**P2.7 — I-69 implementation status and realistic timeline.**
Puentes requires an accurate characterization of the I-69 program status: which segments are built to freeway standards, which require new construction by state, the current EIS status of unprogrammed segments, and the earliest realistic full-corridor completion date under current authorization and funding assumptions. The operational findings (+18% Gulf-Chicago max-flow) should be explicitly conditioned on full corridor completion.

**P2.8 — Air freight competition acknowledgment.**
Hanson and Puentes both note that a portion of high-value NY-LA freight moves by air. The paper should characterize what fraction of the $5.7B reliability cost is attributable to freight segments that compete with air, and whether those segments would actually use I2.0 managed lanes.
