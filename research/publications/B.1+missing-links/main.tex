\title{Missing Links: Gap Analysis of the US Interstate Network}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}
\date{May 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Between 15\% and 20\% of Americans live in counties more than 30 miles from any interstate highway on-ramp: a corrected estimate of 48.8 million people (14.8\%) after removing likely county-centroid artifacts, with an upper bound of 66.5 million (20.4\%) if all gap counties are taken at face value. We present the first systematic population-weighted gap analysis of the US interstate network, using Census Bureau county centroids, TIGER/Line highway geometry, and the ROUTE 12-dimensional corridor scoring framework. We identify four geographic gap zones (Northern Tier, Appalachians, Rural Gulf South, Rural West), classify gap counties into four types, and rank 12 priority proposed corridors by coverage efficiency (gap population served per billion dollars of estimated investment). Gap counties score 41\% higher on the C3 (Economic Opportunity Access) dimension than non-gap counties, confirming that the coverage gap and the economic opportunity gap are geographically co-located. We also acknowledge that prior interstate construction caused severe equity harm to urban communities of color, and propose that any new corridor alignment process include mandatory community impact assessment. Twelve priority corridors phased across three federal authorization cycles (\$71B Phase 1, \$60B Phase 2, \$161B Phase 3) would bring the coverage-deficit population within the 30-mile threshold, targeting 99\% national coverage by 2040.
\end{abstract}
