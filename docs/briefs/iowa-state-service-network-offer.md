# Iowa State Service Network Offer

## Offer

ROUTE designs Iowa's highway system as a service network.

The client brings priorities: cities, freight nodes, rural production regions,
hospitals, universities, airports, river terminals, industrial parks, emergency
routes, and politically important gaps. ROUTE turns those priorities into a
tiered promise model, resilience blueprint, investment package, and dashboard.

## Executive Pitch

Iowa already has roads. The question is what service the system should promise.

ROUTE helps Iowa answer:

- Which places must stay connected?
- Which connections deserve the strongest reliability promise?
- Which rural and production regions need protected access?
- Which terminals and local bottlenecks limit the economy?
- Which failures would be unacceptable?
- Which investments buy the promise first?
- Which dashboard tells leaders whether the promise is being delivered?

## 90-Day Product Package

| Phase | Client Input | ROUTE Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Priority intake | Top cities, freight nodes, rural regions, terminals, hospitals, campuses, resilience concerns, and political constraints | Iowa service-network intake map and promise backlog |
| 2. Tier design | Agreement on what T1, T2, T3, T4, and resilience mean for Iowa | Draft Iowa service hierarchy and tier promise table |
| 3. Promise design | Desired reliability, access, recovery, and terminal outcomes | Candidate service targets by tier and failure mode |
| 4. Resilience blueprint | Known closure, flood, snow, bridge, work-zone, and incident pain points | Critical failure map, alternate-route standards, recovery target menu |
| 5. Investment packaging | Budget posture, delivery capacity, near-term priorities, long-range capital appetite | Quick wins, operating fixes, asset fixes, terminal access, rural access, and capital package sequence |
| 6. Dashboard design | Existing performance measures and leadership reporting needs | Executive dashboard spec for reliability, access, recovery, asset condition, bottlenecks, and package progress |

## Candidate Iowa Promises

These are design promises to refine with the client. They are not legal
guarantees until Iowa chooses ownership, funding, operating rules, and adoption
process.

| Promise | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Top cities stay connected | Des Moines, Cedar Rapids/Iowa City, Quad Cities, Council Bluffs/Omaha, Sioux City, Waterloo/Cedar Falls, and key regional centers are treated as a service system. |
| Freight has a reliability product | Freight corridors, river terminals, rail/intermodal access, industrial parks, and production regions get explicit reliability and bottleneck goals. |
| Rural production is visible | Agricultural, food-processing, energy, and rural hospital access are protected from being erased by traffic-volume-only logic. |
| The last mile is part of the network | Airport, river terminal, rail yard, hospital, university, downtown, and industrial park access gaps are treated as service failures. |
| Failure modes are pre-designed | Flood, snow, work-zone, bridge, incident, and interchange failures get alternate-route and recovery expectations before the crisis. |
| Investment is staged | Iowa gets a sequence: operational fixes first, asset and bottleneck fixes second, larger capital packages only where the promise requires them. |

## What The Client Buys

| Deliverable | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Iowa service hierarchy | Shows how statewide, regional, rural, terminal, and resilience roles fit together. |
| Promise table | Defines what each tier is supposed to deliver. |
| Resilience blueprint | Names unacceptable failures, backup routes, recovery targets, and hardening needs. |
| Investment package sequence | Converts the vision into fundable packages and pilot options. |
| Executive story | Gives leaders a clear service promise for Iowa's people and economy. |
| Operating dashboard spec | Makes the promise measurable after adoption. |

## What ROUTE Is Not Selling

ROUTE is not selling an official Iowa DOT plan, legal SLA guarantee, route
designation, engineering design, construction program, funding commitment,
environmental clearance, right-of-way clearance, or ROI proof.

ROUTE is selling the design system that lets Iowa decide what its highway
network should promise and what it would take to deliver that promise.

## Close

The first meeting does not need every data source. It needs a client answer to
one question:

> What places in Iowa must stay connected, and what failure would be
> unacceptable?

ROUTE turns that answer into a service network.
