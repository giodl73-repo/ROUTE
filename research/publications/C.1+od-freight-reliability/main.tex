\begin{abstract}
We analyze freight reliability on two foundational US highway O-D pairs: New York to Los Angeles (the primary northern transcontinental route via I-80) and Houston to Chicago (the Gulf-to-Midwest corridor via I-45/I-35/I-55). For NY--LA: the binding capacity constraint is Donner Pass at 91,200 vehicles per day maximum, with the route currently operating at peak volume/capacity ratios of 1.86 in the Bay Area segment. Interstate 2.0 managed freight lanes reduce transcontinental transit time from 4.5 days to approximately 3.5 days, with a Planning Time Index improvement from 1.86 to a target of 1.15 --- enabling 48-hour commitment windows for shippers currently requiring 80+ hour buffers. For Houston--Chicago: no direct T1 corridor exists; the three-corridor hop adds 300 miles and two high-variance interchange nodes. I-69 completion would eliminate both the distance penalty and the interchange bottlenecks. Combined, these two O-D pairs account for an estimated \$8.2 billion in annual freight reliability costs that I2.0 investment would materially reduce.
\end{abstract}
