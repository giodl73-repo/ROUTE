\begin{abstract}
The 15 intersections among Tier 1 Primary Arteries in the US interstate network are the highest-value nodes in the national freight graph: points where a disruption on one T1 corridor could theoretically be absorbed by an adjacent T1. In practice, 9 of these 15 intersections have edge k-connectivity of 1 --- a single grade-level interchange that fails during incident, congestion, or construction. We define the diamond interchange zone (50-mile access-controlled zone centered on a T1/T1 junction) as the investment unit that converts a k=1 single-point intersection to k$\geq$3 resilient interchange. Analysis of the 15 T1/T1 intersections shows that \$4.5 billion in diamond interchange investment (average \$300M per junction) would eliminate the k=1 condition at all critical junctions. Priority sequencing places Atlanta (I-75/I-85), Jacksonville (I-10/I-95), and Toledo (I-75/I-90) in Phase 1, representing the highest betweenness product scores in the T1/T1 matrix.
\end{abstract}
