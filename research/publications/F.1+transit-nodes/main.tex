\begin{abstract}
The Interstate 2.0 program --- a \$253 billion upgrade of the US highway system --- is framed as a freight investment. But every T1/T1 diamond hub and T1/T2 regional stop also creates the physical infrastructure for intercity bus service to operate at reliability levels currently unavailable outside the Northeast Corridor. Nine T1/T1 diamond hubs and approximately 50 T1/T2 regional stops bring an estimated 12.4 million transit-dependent Americans within 30 miles of an intercity transit connection they currently do not have. The hub increment investment --- bus platforms, passenger amenities, and passenger parking at locations already built for freight access --- costs approximately \$2 billion on a \$253 billion program: 0.8\% of total I2.0 cost. This hub investment serves transit-dependent travelers at a cost of \$161 per traveler served --- 14 times more efficient than comparable standalone bus terminal construction (\$2,200 per traveler-served). T1/T1 hub locations also align disproportionately with communities scoring high on C3 (Economic Opportunity Access), making the transit layer a significant equity multiplier for the highway investment.
\end{abstract}
