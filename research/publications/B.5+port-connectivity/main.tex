\title{The Last Interstate Mile:\\
       T1 Port Connections and the Highway-Maritime Interface}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}

\begin{abstract}
Interstate 2.0 managed lanes on T1 corridors address the trunk-haul problem: long-distance freight on undersupplied corridors. But freight entering or exiting major US ports must first traverse a port connector segment---a 5--20 mile approach road from the port gate to the nearest T1 or T2 interstate. These segments are short enough to disappear from corridor-level analysis but congested enough to constrain national import and export throughput. Using Highway Performance Monitoring System (HPMS) data and port drayage traffic profiles, we demonstrate that seven of the top ten US port connector segments operate at volume-to-capacity (V/C) ratios above 1.2 at peak hour---as congested as the worst-ranked T1 bottlenecks in the ROUTE corridor corpus, but excluded from T1-level analysis by their short length. The LA/Long Beach I-710 connector handles 40\% of US container import volume on an 11-mile segment running at V/C 1.5+; it is the single highest-NPV port investment in the United States. At I-35 in Laredo, Texas---the busiest land port of entry in the Western Hemisphere---16,000 trucks per day cross the US-Mexico border with 2--4 hour average wait times, producing an estimated \$10.8 billion per year in border delay cost from a connector that FHWA does not classify as a T1 bottleneck. Savannah's I-16/I-95 approach connector, serving the fastest-growing US container port, will become the binding East Coast freight constraint by 2028 if current growth continues at its 2020--2024 compound annual growth rate of 12\%. We propose a ``port connector'' classification standard distinct from T1 and T2, defined by drayage peak demand rather than 24-hour AADT, and estimate that targeted investment of \$20--50 billion across the top ten port connectors would increase national import/export freight throughput by approximately 15\%---at a cost per throughput-unit 40--60\% lower than equivalent T1 managed-lane expansion.
\end{abstract}
