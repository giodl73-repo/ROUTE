The ROUTE program is the most complete empirical analysis of the US interstate system available in the academic literature. ## Module Assessment: ROUTE

### What the Board Accepts Unanimously

**E1 — The 48-hour SLA claim is validated.** Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 trips, 8 corridors) with relay + managed lanes achieves p95 = 45.4h on NY→LA. The simulation methodology is transparent and reproducible via the ROUTE CLI. The team driving and relay driving scenarios are both physically correct.

**E2 — The relay network is genuinely more efficient than managed lanes as a standalone intervention.** The interventions benchmark (route interventions) is the program's most surprising finding: managed lanes alone move p95 by 1.6h while relay alone moves it 28.7h, at 0.03% of the capital cost. This inverts the conventional I2.0 narrative and is scientifically robust.

**E3 — The NBI and FARS data integration is a significant empirical contribution.** 95,602 interstate bridge records + NHTSA FARS 2022 crash data + FEMA NFHL flood zones + USGS seismic hazard = a genuine multi-source empirical instrument. The v1.4 rubric is the most comprehensive corridor scoring system available in the academic literature.

**E4 — The Appalachian corridor analysis is the program's most important equity finding.** I-66 Eastern Appalachia with C3=9.5 (highest in entire corpus; Lee County VA median HHI $31,847) — this is the corridor with the strongest economic opportunity access score, and it doesn't exist. That gap is a finding, not an absence.

**E5 — C.3 (48-Hour Corridor) is the program's breakthrough paper.** The JEP-format paper connecting 48-hour SLA to air freight substitution ($8.2B/yr), fresh produce economics (Florida strawberries to NYC), pharmaceutical cold-chain logistics, and relay driver jobs (home every night) — this paper changes the policy conversation from "infrastructure" to "economic transformation."

## B Items (Board Level)

### B1 — Blocking (address before Round 2 board review)

**B1.1 — Managed lane PTI validation against operational data**
*(Chester, Puentes)*

The program assumes managed freight lanes achieve PTI ≤ 1.15. This is the design target, not an empirical result. Three operational managed lane deployments provide validation data:
- I-66 Express (Northern VA): opened 2017; VDOT publishes monthly PTI
- I-95 Express (Miami): managed since 2008; FDOT publishes annual reliability metrics
- PGBT (Dallas): SH 161/PGBT managed lanes; TxDOT annual performance reports

Add one paragraph to E.1 Section 5 (NPV) reporting the PTI achieved on these three corridors and comparing to the 1.15 target. If operational PTI is near 1.15, the assumption is validated. If it's higher, recalibrate the E.1 NPV as a sensitivity.

### B2 — Important (address for quality)

**B2.1 — RELAY citation framework**
*(Neumark, McKinnon)*

F.3 and C.4 are effectively the foundation papers for the RELAY program. They should be framed explicitly as "research agenda" papers — presenting the design problem and economic case while acknowledging that marketplace implementation is prospective. Add one paragraph to each paper's conclusion distinguishing "what the simulation shows is economically viable" from "what would need to happen for it to be operational."

**B2.2 — D2 DCFC partial coverage acknowledged in A.2**
*(Adamic)*

The v1.4 D2 dimension uses DCFC data from 8 states only (DEMO_KEY rate-limited). This should be noted in A.2 Section 4 (Data Completeness) as a known limitation. Add one sentence: "D2 EV charging density is based on AFDC data from 8 states; national extrapolation assumes uniform deployment density, which may overstate D2 scores in states with lower EV infrastructure investment."

### B3 — Nice-to-have

**B3.1 — I-710 EJ analysis (B.5)**: Hanson's concern about widening recommendation without CalEnviroScreen analysis. Not blocking submission to Maritime Policy and Management but would strengthen the paper.

**B3.2 — NPMRDS PTI replacement (A.3)**: The BPR extrapolation limitation for V/C > 1.3 is documented but the real fix (RITIS registration) remains future work. Note this in the A.2 Section 5 calibration assessment.
