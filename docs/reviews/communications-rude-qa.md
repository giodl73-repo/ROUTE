# Communications Rude Q&A

## Purpose

This prep sheet gives presenters and reviewers a hostile-question script for
the Interstate 2.0 communications package.

The point is not to sound polished. The point is to catch places where a deck,
brief, report, demo, map, generated entry, score, simulation, or review packet
might accidentally imply more proof than ROUTE currently has.

This Q&A is an internal rehearsal artifact. It does not create an official plan,
construction recommendation, operating guarantee, ROI finding, eligibility
finding, compliance finding, endorsement, approval, or public-readiness claim.

## Core Rude Q&A

| Rude Question | Passing Answer |
|---|---|
| Is this an actual official Interstate 2.0 plan? | No. It is an evidence-bounded research and planning system. It shows how claims become reviewable, downgraded, or held. |
| Are you saying these corridors should be built? | No. ROUTE does not make construction recommendations without source packs, role review, cost basis, state/DOT feasibility, and claim promotion. |
| So the maps are basically proof? | No. Maps are structural visuals and inspection aids. They do not prove SLA readiness, asset readiness, funding need, or project readiness. |
| Where are the real ROI numbers? | Held. The ROI package defines the evidence contract first: cost basis, price year, benefit categories, uncertainty, excluded benefits, and source review. |
| Is the 48-hour freight promise guaranteed? | No. It is a service-network doctrine and planning target. Operating SLA proof remains gated by reliability, flow, stops, relay, and source evidence. |
| Why should anyone trust the score? | They should not trust only the total. They should inspect the 16 dimensions, confidence labels, estimated flags, sources, and sensitivity posture. |
| Is `route invest` a funding plan? | No. It is a rough allocation experiment using coarse assumptions. It is useful for review questions, not capital programming. |
| Are relay hubs proven to solve labor, EV charging, or autonomy? | No. Relay hubs are a strong operating-model story, but labor, EV, AV, utilization, siting, and safety claims remain heuristic or gated. |
| Does a generated report prove anything? | No. Generated corpus/report artifacts are inspection surfaces. Provenance shows how the draft was made, not that the claim is true. |
| Are rural access claims just decoration? | No. T3/T4 access, coverage gaps, terminal proof, feeder columns, and role review keep rural/access pressure visible, but specific access outcomes remain gated. |
| Why not just publish this? | Because an internal rehearsal pass is not external readiness. External use still needs a named venue, populated source-backed fixture, role review, prohibited-claim scan, and readiness validation. |
| Who disagrees with this? | ROUTE is designed to preserve dissent. `.roles` review should surface freight, rural, state DOT, environmental, transit-dependent, delivery, and numeracy objections. |
| Are you hiding behind "evidence-bounded" because the claims are weak? | No. Evidence boundaries are the product discipline: they show what is implemented, heuristic, held, or source-needed before anyone mistakes a story for proof. |
| What would make this pass DOT review? | Command provenance, source packs, graph/scoring assumptions, confidence labels, claim-promotion trace, role review, and clear downgrade/hold decisions for unsupported claims. |
| What is the one sentence you can safely say? | ROUTE makes Interstate 2.0 infrastructure claims inspectable, refinable, and evidence-bounded before they become public, funding, or policy claims. |

## Round-Specific Rude Questions

| Round | Question | Passing Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Intra-state regional | Why should local governments care if this is not a project list? | Because ROUTE gives local and regional stakeholders a way to attach source-backed requirements, objections, access needs, and constraints before a claim hardens. |
| Intra-state regional | Are you using national freight language to steamroll local impacts? | No. Local impacts, non-driving access, environmental/community health, delivery feasibility, and rural needs stay visible through role review and held claims. |
| State | Are you asking the state DOT to bless this? | No. The current ask is review of assumptions, source gaps, feasibility blockers, and requirements that would change or hold claims. |
| State | Does a state value brief imply state commitment? | No. It is an intake and framing surface. It does not represent agency adoption, funding, compliance, or project readiness. |
| Multi-state regional | Who owns cross-border commitments? | Nobody yet. Regional packets are evidence handoffs and coordination surfaces, not governance commitments or compact language. |
| Multi-state regional | Are route labels hiding state-scope gaps? | They should not. Bundle/member/stitch identity and evidence state scope are the required review objects. Label-only claims should be held. |
| Congressional | Is this just a national construction wish list? | No. The package is a method for making infrastructure claims auditable before any program, project, or funding claim is promoted. |
| Congressional | Can an elected official use this to claim local funding is coming? | No. Safe language is limited to research, requirements, evidence gaps, and staged review. Funding and eligibility claims remain held. |
| DOT review | Can we reproduce and challenge the artifacts? | That is the standard. Reviewers should see commands, source paths, confidence labels, bundle ids, generated outputs, role decisions, and held blockers. |
| DOT review | What happens when a DOT reviewer rejects a claim? | The claim is revised, downgraded, held, or split into source-needed tasks. The rejection becomes part of the review record, not a presentation problem to hide. |

## Feature-Specific Pressure Lines

| Surface | Rude Question | Passing Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Split decks | Why is ROUTE hidden from the solution deck? | The public deck sells the Interstate 2.0 idea; the technology deck explains ROUTE as the inspection/refinement engine. The split prevents brand/tool confusion. |
| Evidence posture | Why so many held claims? | Because held claims are visible evidence debt. The package should show what survives review and what cannot be promoted yet. |
| Source operations | Are your sources complete? | No blanket claim. Source posture is artifact-specific: source-needed, cached, archived, repeat-window, fixture-backed, or held. |
| Optimizer | Did the optimizer find the answer? | No. It creates candidate artifacts, constraints, holds, and review questions under current assumptions. |
| Standards/Blueprint | Are these standards ready to fund? | No. Standards and Blueprint packages are downgraded or held until proof, cost, source, and role gates close. |
| Asset condition | Can you promise the network can support this service? | No. Pavement, bridge, WIM, rest, local maintenance, and source-access debt remain visible blockers where unresolved. |
| Simulation/game | Did the simulation prove the system works? | No. Simulation and game outputs are scenario, teaching, and stress-test surfaces unless separately source-backed and validated. |
| Release/publication | Is the browser/game/map package ready for public use? | No blanket claim. Public readiness remains gated by release manifest, L1/L2 validation, browser/game evidence, and external-readiness review. |

## Presenter Rule

When a rude question lands, the presenter should answer in this order:

1. Name the current posture: implemented, story-ready, heuristic, gated, or held.
2. Name the artifact that supports that posture.
3. State the blocked claim plainly.
4. Name the next evidence step.

If the presenter cannot do all four, the answer should be:

> We should hold that claim until the source, command, and role-review path is
> clear.

## Pass Criteria

The rude Q&A passes internal rehearsal only when:

- every answer preserves evidence posture;
- no answer implies official adoption, construction, guaranteed service,
  numeric ROI, eligibility, compliance, endorsement, approval, or public-readiness claims;
- source-backed fixture blockers remain visible;
- state, regional, congressional, and DOT audiences get different enough
  answers to match their authority and risks;
- the presenter can convert hostile objections into next evidence steps.
