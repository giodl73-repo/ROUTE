# Canada Internal Proof Brief

## Media-Safe Summary

The Canada pilot is internally proven as a source-backed, evidence-gated ROUTE
adapter workflow. It shows that ROUTE can move a non-U.S. country pilot through
source custody, parser-shaped fixtures, role review, held service targets, and a
proof closeout.

## What It Proves

| Area | Safe Statement |
|---|---|
| Portability | Canada is a working internal example of the source-to-fixture-to-review workflow. |
| Links | Canada link rows are source-derived no-geometry internal fixture rows. |
| Nodes | Canada node rows carry selected public port source-custody candidates for Vancouver, Montreal, and Halifax. |
| Needs | Canada need rows use bounded road-system and trade-corridor vocabulary. |
| Targets | Canada service targets remain held planning assumptions. |

## What It Does Not Prove

- official Canadian network status;
- route designation;
- Transport Canada, provincial, or port approval;
- geometry, topology, or map-overlay proof;
- terminal performance, node completeness, road-access adequacy, or throughput;
- construction readiness;
- guaranteed SLA, travel-time proof, or delivery commitment;
- ROI, eligibility, compliance, endorsement, validation, public readiness, or
  external readiness.

## Quote-Safe Answer

Use:

> ROUTE's Canada pilot is internally proven as a source-backed,
> evidence-gated adapter workflow; external validation and official transport
> claims remain held.

Avoid language that turns the internal proof into certification, adoption, or
network validation.

## External Review Path

The next Canada step is selecting a real external review lane and venue, not
claiming external validation. The scoped pathway is recorded in
`docs/reviews/international-canada-external-review-pathway-001.md`.

Safe wording:

> Canada has a scoped pathway for future external review; external validation
> has not started.
