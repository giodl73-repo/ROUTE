\title{Resilience Holes: Compound Exposure in the National Highway Network}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}

\begin{abstract}
We identify 11 corridors in the US interstate network where structural isolation (B1 score $>$ 7) and environmental vulnerability (D1 score $>$ 6) co-occur simultaneously --- compound resilience holes where a single weather event eliminates national freight throughput with no viable rerouting option. The compound exposure concept differs from single-dimension risk: a corridor with high structural isolation but low environmental exposure is a contingency risk that may not materialize; one with high exposure but good redundancy is manageable. Compound holes are where both conditions apply simultaneously. The top 4 extreme-compound corridors (B1 $>$ 8, D1 $>$ 7) account for an estimated \$3.1 billion in annual freight disruption cost. Investment in compound-exposure corridors yields higher NPV than single-dimension investments because a single project (e.g., Donner freight tunnel) simultaneously addresses structural isolation and environmental vulnerability.
\end{abstract}
