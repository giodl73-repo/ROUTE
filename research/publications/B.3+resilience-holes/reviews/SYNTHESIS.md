# Review Synthesis — B.3+resilience-holes Round 1

## Headline Assessment

The compound resilience hole concept is a genuine and important contribution to the transportation infrastructure literature — the first rigorous operationalization of the non-additive character of co-occurring structural isolation and environmental vulnerability in highway networks. All five reviewers accept the conceptual framework. The paper is valuable; the panel recommends revision.

The quantitative case rests on the Donner Pass cost estimate ($1.6B/year, $15.8B NPV), and two reviewers (McKinnon, Elefteriadou) have identified specific, material errors in the calculation that reduce the headline NPV figure. The 2050 projection claim (Gulf Coast overtaking Donner) is asserted without showing the NOAA SLR → SFHA → D1 calculation chain (Chester). The PROTECT reform policy pathway needs timeline specificity (Puentes). The geographic community dependency angle is absent (Hanson).

**The panel mean is 2.6/4 (≥ 2.5 threshold). The minimum is 2/4 (meets floor). The paper advances to revision.**

The P1 items must be addressed before recheck. P1.1 and P1.2 are quantitative corrections; P1.3 is a methodology transparency requirement; P1.4 is a policy pathway specification.

---

## What the Panel Agrees On (Earned Stakes)

**E1 — The compound resilience hole concept is valid and the non-additive framing is correct.** All five reviewers accept the core conceptual contribution. The B1×D1 product metric is a defensible first-order prioritization tool. The three-way counterfactual framing (high B1/low D1, low B1/high D1, high B1/high D1) is the clearest explanation of non-additive compound risk in the transportation literature. This earned stake is secure.

**E2 — The 11-corridor compound exposure catalog (Table 2) is the paper's most durable empirical contribution.** All reviewers find the corpus of compound exposure corridors credible and immediately useful for practitioners. The list will be cited independently of the NPV analysis. Chester, Hanson, and Puentes all note that Table 2 alone would justify publication in a shorter form.

**E3 — The NPV superiority argument for compound investments is structurally correct.** The formal characterization (NPV_compound = NPV_B1-fix + NPV_D1-fix - NPV_overlap, where overlap is negative because a single capital cost purchases both benefit streams) is accepted by all reviewers as economically valid. The Donner tunnel three-way comparison (tunnel vs. US-50 alternate vs. snowshed) is the right analytical structure. The conclusion — compound investment dominates either single-dimension investment — is directionally correct even if the specific NPV numbers require revision.

**E4 — The PROTECT program reform recommendation is the paper's strongest policy contribution.** Four of five reviewers (Chester, Puentes, McKinnon, Hanson) explicitly endorse this recommendation. The observation that current PROTECT criteria are D1-equivalent and systematically underweight B1 network topology is accurate and important. This recommendation should be the paper's policy centerpiece. Puentes adds the implementation pathway detail; Puentes's concern is resolvable with a paragraph, not a new analysis.

## Priority Items

### P1 — Blocking (must address before recheck)

**P1.1 — Correct the waiting cost rate in the Donner disruption cost calculation.**
*(Elefteriadou, primary; McKinnon, supporting)*
The paper applies the full ATRI operating rate ($225/hr) to waiting trucks. The correct waiting cost is approximately $91/hr (driver time + idle fuel + overhead). Correcting this for the 0.6 waiting fraction reduces the D1 benefit component from approximately $700M/year to approximately $280M/year. Total Donner annual benefit falls from $1.6B/year to approximately $1.18B/year. The 30-year NPV at 7% falls from $15.8B to approximately $11.6B; CBR falls from 5.75:1 to approximately 4.0:1. **All affected NPV and CBR figures in Tables 3, 4, and the abstract must be recalculated and corrected.** The investment case remains strongly positive after correction; the paper should state this explicitly.

**P1.2 — Add a sensitivity table for the Donner NPV estimate.**
*(McKinnon, primary; Chester, supporting)*
The paper must add a sensitivity table showing NPV as a function of ±50% variation in each key input: truck count (AADTT), closure frequency (events/year), average closure duration (hours), rerouting behavioral fraction, and discount rate (5%/7%/9%). The table should show the range of NPV estimates and confirm that the NPV sign is positive across all ±50% scenarios. If any scenario produces a negative NPV, that must be discussed. This is a standard requirement for infrastructure investment papers making multi-billion-dollar NPV claims.

**P1.3 — Show the 2050 D1 projection calculation chain.**
*(Chester, primary)*
The paper states Gulf Coast I-10 D1 will rise from 8.4 to 9.1 by 2050, and Donner D1 will rise from 7.8 to 8.5. These numbers are presented as projections but the methodology is not shown. Add a methods appendix or supplementary section showing: (a) the NOAA SLR scenario used (low/intermediate/high); (b) how the SLR value translates to SFHA boundary expansion for the specific Gulf Coast I-10 segments (this requires hydraulic modeling assumptions — state what they are); (c) how the revised SFHA boundaries translate to a revised D1 score under the ROUTE rubric. For Donner, show how IPCC RCP 4.5 precipitation-intensity projections translate to revised closure frequency and/or severity, and then to revised D1. Without this, the 2050 priority reordering claim (Section 6.4) is not publishable.

**P1.4 — Add PROTECT reform implementation pathway specificity.**
*(Puentes, primary)*
The PROTECT reform recommendation must include: (a) the specific mechanism — a revised PROTECT NOFO with compound score as a weighted evaluation criterion; (b) the timeline — FHWA NOFO revision cycle, typically 60-day comment period, FY2027 next anticipated round; (c) the immediate action recommended — submit ROUTE compound score methodology to FHWA's Office of Planning, Environment, and Realty for consideration in the next NOFO development. This is a paragraph addition, not a new analysis.

### P2 — Important (address for publication quality)

**P2.1 — Add lifecycle O&M cost to the NPV comparison.**
*(Chester, primary)*
Table 3 presents capital costs only. Add a row for present value of O&M costs over 30 years for each investment alternative. Donner freight tunnel: ~$25-35M/year O&M (ventilation, lighting, emergency systems, tolling infrastructure if applicable). At 7% discount rate, PV of O&M over 30 years is approximately $290-410M. Snowshed alternative: ~$5M/year, PV ~$58M. The tunnel likely still wins the NPV comparison after adding O&M, but the paper must show this. Present a corrected Table 3 with capital cost + PV(O&M) as the total cost column.

**P2.2 — Characterize the community populations dependent on compound-exposure corridors.**
*(Hanson, primary)*
Add a paragraph for each of the top 4 corridors describing the community dependency beyond freight: Donner (Truckee, Lake Tahoe communities, estimated population isolated during closures), Gulf Coast (coastal Louisiana communities east of Baton Rouge), Snoqualmie (Yakima Valley agricultural communities). This is not a new analysis — census population within 30 miles of each corridor segment, cross-referenced with closure data. It strengthens the equity and public welfare case for investment.

**P2.3 — Add B1 geographic sensitivity discussion.**
*(Hanson, primary)*
Add a paragraph in Section 2 or 4 explaining how geographic barrier character (altitude, relief, pass geology, coastal topography) translates to B1 scores. Specifically: why Donner (7,227 ft, 310 miles to nearest alternate) scores B1 = 8.3 while Snoqualmie (3,022 ft, 60 miles to alternate) scores 7.6. This makes the scoring transparent and allows readers to generalize the methodology to corridors not in the current corpus.

**P2.4 — Verify the 49 U.S.C. § 70101 citation in Section 6.3.**
*(Puentes, primary)*
The National Critical Highway Infrastructure designation argument cites 49 U.S.C. § 70101 et seq. as providing specific designation authorities including "mandatory federal funding participation above normal NHS apportionment formula." This citation should be verified by a transportation law specialist before publication. If the cited authority does not support the specific funding claim described, the Section 6.3 recommendation must be reformulated.

### P3 — Enhancements (for a stronger paper)

**P3.1 — Add B1/D1 NPV decomposition for each portfolio corridor.**
*(Chester, primary)*
For each of the four portfolio corridors, show how much of the total annual benefit comes from the B1 fix (avoided rerouting cost) and how much from the D1 fix (avoided waiting cost). This validates the compound investment claim beyond the Donner case: if Gulf Coast I-10's benefit is predominantly D1-driven (with a minor B1 component), it should be acknowledged that Gulf Coast is "D1-primary with B1 supplemental" rather than a fully bilateral compound investment.

**P3.2 — Consider joint highway-rail compound exposure analysis.**
*(Hanson, primary)*
For Donner Pass and Gulf Coast I-10, where UP transcontinental rail and coastal rail run in geographic alignment with the highway, consider whether the investment case strengthens with a joint highway-rail framing. A Donner tunnel that accommodates both highway and rail freight addresses compound exposure across both modes simultaneously and would attract FRA funding alongside FHWA investment.

**P3.3 — Address pre-adaptation baseline problem.**
*(Elefteriadou, primary; McKinnon, supporting)*
The paper assumes current truck counts on compound-exposure corridors represent the full demand that would use these routes. But shippers may have already adapted supply chains to known closure risk (avoiding I-80 Sierra crossing for JIT loads, using rail for Sierra crossing during high-closure seasons). If so, the current AADTT understates the traffic that would materialize without closure risk, and the tunnel benefit is higher than current truck counts suggest. Acknowledge this adaptation baseline issue and note that the AADTT-based estimates are conservative for this reason.
