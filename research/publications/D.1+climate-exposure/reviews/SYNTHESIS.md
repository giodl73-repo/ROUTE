## Headline Assessment

Strong coastal flood analysis with a policy-ready PROTECT program critique, undermined by a composite metric that mixes incommensurable sub-scores for winter and flood corridors. The paper is publishable at *Nature Climate Change* in revised form if it addresses the composite metric problem and adds methodological humility to the 2050 projection layer. One reviewer (McKinnon) scores it 2/4 on the grounds that the category error is structural; the other four reviewers find the paper's core contributions valuable and score it 3/4.

---

## Earned Stakes (E1–E4)

**E1 — The D1 consecutive-mile weighting is the right operational metric for coastal corridors.**
All five reviewers accept that contiguous SFHA exposure is operationally worse than equivalent total exposure distributed across non-contiguous segments. Chester validates the systems logic; Hanson finds the spatial concept sound; Puentes calls it actionable for PROTECT rulemaking. This finding stands without revision.

**E2 — The PROTECT program allocation formula is systematically mismatched to operational closure risk for the Louisiana corridor.**
Chester, Puentes, and Eberts all accept this finding. The specific critique — that FHWA allocates by total SFHA miles while consecutive mileage is the operationally relevant variable — is both technically correct and policy-relevant. This is the paper's most important applied contribution.

**E3 — Gulf Coast I-10 LA (D1=8.4) is the highest-risk corridor in the current corpus.**
The data supporting this finding — 127 consecutive SFHA miles — is the strongest in the paper, sourced from a well-specified NFHL API query. No reviewer disputes the ranking for current conditions.

**E4 — The 2050 priority order shift (Gulf Coast LA most critical climate adaptation investment) is directionally credible.**
Three reviewers accept the directional finding despite reservations about projection precision. McKinnon does not dispute the coastal projection per se; Chester raises the subsidence gap as a reason the D1 increase from 8.4 to 9.1 may be an underestimate rather than an overestimate.

## P1 Blocking Items

**P1.1 — Resolve the composite metric category error.**
The winter closure proxy (Caltrans/WSDOT closure days) is not dimensionally comparable to the SFHA flood zone metric. The paper must either: (a) develop a physically grounded winter exposure metric (e.g., PRISM accumulated precipitation as freezing precipitation per unit elevation, or atmospheric river event frequency) that is analogous to SFHA physical geography; (b) present coastal and winter corridors in separate D1 sub-scores and not produce a cross-category composite ranking; or (c) restrict the paper's scope to coastal flood corridors and defer winter corridors to a companion paper. All other revisions are secondary to this.

**P1.2 — Add subsidence to the 2050 coastal projection.**
Louisiana subsidence rates (8–12mm/yr, USGS) substantially increase effective relative SLR for Gulf Coast LA corridor segments. The 2050 D1 projection (8.4→9.1) should be presented as a lower bound, with a bounding sensitivity using NOAA SLR + subsidence. If subsidence elevates the 2050 estimate to D1≥9.5, the policy urgency framing changes.

**P1.3 — Report NFHL coverage completeness per corridor.**
The paper must report the percentage of corridor miles with effective FIRM panel coverage vs. unmapped or preliminary status for each corridor analyzed. Corridors with gaps >10% of total mileage should be flagged; analysis should discuss directional bias in D1 scoring under incomplete coverage.

---

## P2 Important Items

**P2.1 — Quantify the PROTECT allocation gap in dollars.**
"Systematically underweights" must be translated to a dollar amount. Under a consecutive-mile formula vs. the current total-mile formula, what does Louisiana receive per year — and what is the delta? Without this, the policy critique is qualitative.

**P2.2 — Add inter-annual variance reporting for winter closure proxy.**
Report: (1) the time window used for Caltrans/WSDOT closure frequency; (2) inter-annual coefficient of variation for each winter corridor; (3) sensitivity of D1 winter corridor scores across high/average/low precipitation years.

**P2.3 — Identify PROTECT regulatory vs. statutory boundary.**
The paper recommends PROTECT formula reform without specifying whether the formula is regulatory (changeable by FHWA rulemaking) or statutory (requires legislation). Clarify which path applies and its timeline.

**P2.4 — Report post-Ida NFHL status for Gulf Coast LA corridor.**
Verify whether the NFHL panels used for Gulf Coast LA reflect post-Ida (2021) remapping or pre-Ida boundaries. If pre-Ida, note that D1=8.4 may understate current SFHA extent pending FIRM update.

**P2.5 — Scope the Montana/Wyoming I-90 segments.**
Eberts asks why I-90 east of Snoqualmie (Montana/Wyoming segments) is absent from the analysis. If excluded by a ranking threshold, state that. If those segments score below the top-5 cutoff, report their approximate D1 scores to establish the geographic scope of winter corridor risk.
