# The 48-Hour Freight Promise

## Research Question

How should ROUTE explain the T1 48h/36h freight promise as the national
reliability spine without implying guaranteed service, publication-grade SLA
proof, or corridor-specific investment readiness?

## Decision This Supports

This report supports the communications decision to make the 48-hour freight
promise the most legible economic hook for T1.

The promise should be used as a planning doctrine and funder story: what would
it mean for the country to buy national reliability? It should not be used as an
operating guarantee until direct reliability evidence, relay assumptions,
incident assumptions, sensitivity runs, and role review close.

## Executive Thesis

The public understands time. Shippers, carriers, states, and funders understand
the difference between "there is a highway" and "this network can support a
reliable arrival window."

The 48-hour freight promise turns Interstate 2.0 from a map into a service
question:

```text
What would the national road network need to do
to make premium long-distance freight reliability credible?
```

In ROUTE doctrine, T1 exists to test that question. It is not the longest road
or the most famous interstate. It is the national reliability spine where
48h/36h promise pairs create obligations for routes, stops, relay hubs,
interchange resilience, alternate paths, and evidence.

## Findings

### ROUTE-48H-01 — Time is the clearest funding language.

**Source references:** `docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 106-125;
`docs/reports/route-evidence-posture.md` lines 48-64.

**Observed constraint:** ROUTE identifies freight reliability as the most
sellable economic outcome because it converts road investment into a service
promise. The evidence posture remains story-ready/heuristic, not proof-grade.

**Implication:** The 48-hour promise should anchor the T1 story, but every
public use needs "planning target" language.

**Confidence:** High for communications; heuristic for operations.

### ROUTE-48H-02 — T1 promises are portfolio obligations, not route fame.

**Source references:** `docs/sla-promise-portfolio.md` lines 5-25 and 37-52.

**Observed constraint:** A route earns a tier because it helps meet a promise
horizon. T1 pairs qualify through national market value, freight conversion
story, reusable segments, geographic coverage, relay/resilience obligation, and
auditable route/stop chains.

**Implication:** The report should not say "make the longest routes T1." It
should say T1 is a disciplined promise portfolio.

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-48H-03 — The cut line must be reviewable.

**Source references:** `docs/sla-promise-portfolio.md` lines 54-72 and 74-90.

**Observed constraint:** The cut line is where another promise pair no longer
changes the national spine enough to justify the route and stop budget it
consumes. Required routes and stops are audit claims that trigger regeneration
when changed.

**Implication:** The T1 story should emphasize disciplined selection and
reviewable omissions rather than a hidden master map.

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-48H-04 — Lower tiers can pressure T1 only through named dependencies.

**Source references:** `docs/sla-promise-portfolio.md` lines 110-126.

**Observed constraint:** T2/T3/T4 pressure can ask T1 to reconsider a line or
stop only when it names a 48h/36h pair, selected T1 stop/transfer, or topology
repair. Publication-grade SLA claims remain held until observed reliability data
or a validated model exists.

**Implication:** Interstate 2.0 can be refinable without becoming arbitrary:
lower-tier needs can change T1, but only through auditable dependencies.

**Confidence:** High.

### ROUTE-48H-05 — Relay hubs are the operating bridge for 48-hour service.

**Source references:** `docs/reports/relay-hubs-aviation-model-report.md`;
`docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 113-120.

**Observed constraint:** Relay, empty backhaul, and hub operations are promising
but heuristic. OD reliability, relay buffer, incident assumptions, operating
assumptions, and industry review are needed before stronger claims.

**Implication:** The 48-hour promise should be paired with relay hubs as a pilot
path, not proof that the service is already operational.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

### ROUTE-48H-06 — Managed lanes and interchange fixes are gated feature claims.

**Source references:** `docs/research-conclusions.md` lines 113-125;
`docs/reports/interstate-2-0-doctrine-report.md` lines 105-118.

**Observed constraint:** Managed freight lanes are a gated claim requiring
corridor-specific PTI, demand, cost, and delivery proof. Interchange resilience
and T1 reliability claims are heuristic until site/scenario evidence closes.

**Implication:** Say "T1 is where these improvements should be tested." Do not
say "these improvements are justified" for named corridors.

**Confidence:** High for boundary.

## What The 48-Hour Promise Means

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public story | The national network should promise more than pavement; it should promise reliability. |
| T1 doctrine | T1 is the national spine shaped by 48h/36h promise pairs. |
| Operating question | What stops, relay hubs, alternates, interchanges, and managed facilities make the promise credible? |
| Evidence question | Which reliability data, relay assumptions, incident scenarios, and sensitivity runs are needed? |
| Funding question | What studies, pilots, and decision packages can move the claim from heuristic to promotable? |

## Safe Language

| Use This | Avoid This |
|---|---|
| "The 48-hour promise is a planning target for national freight reliability." | "The network guarantees 48-hour delivery." |
| "T1 is where the country should test and buy reliability." | "This corridor is proven T1-ready." |
| "Relay hubs can make timed service more operational." | "Relay hubs make 48-hour freight solved." |
| "Managed lanes and interchanges are candidate improvement families." | "Managed lanes are justified here." |
| "The next ask is evidence and pilots." | "The next ask is construction approval." |

## Evidence Needed To Promote The Claim

| Evidence Need | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Observed reliability / PTI source pack | Moves timed promise from planning target toward operating evidence. |
| OD lane and market evidence | Shows which movements actually need premium timed service. |
| Relay buffer assumptions | Defines driver exchange, dwell, charging, staging, and handoff feasibility. |
| Incident and closure scenarios | Tests whether the promise survives adversity. |
| Sensitivity runs | Shows whether the T1 spine is stable under top-10, top-25, top-40, budget, and disruption assumptions. |
| Cost and delivery evidence | Prevents reliability claims from implying construction readiness. |
| Role review | Ensures freight, state delivery, rural access, community health, climate, and numeracy concerns remain visible. |

## Pilot Concepts

| Pilot | What It Tests | Claim Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| T1 promise source pack | Whether selected 48h/36h pairs have enough source evidence for stronger scenarios. | Does not prove delivery windows. |
| Relay-backed lane study | Whether relay buffers could support a named OD service hypothesis. | Does not prove labor, safety, EV, or AV outcomes. |
| Interchange resilience scenario | Whether a T1/T1 node creates unacceptable fragility under failure. | Does not prove a flyover or construction design. |
| Managed freight lane evidence package | Whether reliability, demand, cost, and delivery facts justify deeper study. | Does not prove positive ROI. |
| T1 sensitivity sweep | Whether the selected spine changes under different promise budgets and assumptions. | Does not produce final route rankings. |

### Prototype Behind A Compatibility Boundary

| Prototype | Owner | Validation |
|---|---|---|

| T1 budget sensitivity report. | route-cli / route-network | Generated output comparing selected spine under promise-budget variants. |
| Relay-backed OD scenario fixture. | route-sim / route-cli | Scenario artifact showing relay assumptions and evidence label impact. |

### Reject Or Defer

| Claim / Action | Reason |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed 48-hour national delivery. | Observed reliability and validated model evidence are not closed. |
| Named corridor is T1 construction-ready. | Corridor-specific demand, cost, delivery, and role review are not closed. |
| Managed freight lane has positive ROI. | ROI/cost source pack and numeracy review are not closed. |
| Relay hubs make 48-hour freight operational today. | Relay operations, labor, charging, dwell, and safety assumptions are not closed. |

## Non-Goals

- This report does not prove any route, corridor, or hub can meet 48-hour service.
- This report does not estimate travel times, costs, ROI, demand, or throughput.
- This report does not select construction projects.
- This report does not claim official adoption or operating guarantees.

## Gate

Decision: pass_with_risk

Rationale: The 48-hour freight promise is ready as the T1 communications hook
and planning doctrine. It remains heuristic for operating proof and gated for
named corridor, managed-lane, interchange, relay, ROI, construction, or official
claims.
