## Headline Assessment

The paper identifies a real and under-studied problem — port connector segments as the binding freight capacity constraint — and frames it with a coherent investment thesis. The empirical structure is sound, the Savannah trajectory analysis is the field-strongest section, and the failure-mode taxonomy is a usable analytical contribution. Two issues block promotion: the K_port factor underpinning the V/C analysis lacks empirical or HCM7-derived backing, and the I-710 recommendation proceeds as if the Wilmington/West Long Beach environmental justice context does not exist. Both are addressable in revision. No reviewer recommends rejection; the mean score (2.6/4) reflects genuine methodological and equity gaps, not a weak thesis.

## P1 Blocking Items (must resolve before re-review)

**P1.1 — K_port empirical calibration**
The K_port=0.12–0.14 peak hour factor is the paper's core methodological innovation and underlies every V/C result. It has no empirical source and no HCM7 derivation. Resolution requires one of: (a) empirical calibration from POLA/POLB hourly gate transaction data (publicly available at pola.org); or (b) analytical derivation from HCM7 Section 12 (managed-demand freeway facilities) using gate appointment arrival distributions. Elefteriadou notes the actual value from gate data may be higher (0.18–0.22 at gate-open peak), which would strengthen rather than weaken the paper's central finding. Additionally: specify the queuing model used for the I-710 $1.86B delay cost (M/D/c, BPR, or volume-delay function) and provide that derivation in a methods appendix.

**P1.2 — Equity dimension for I-710**
The I-710 Long Beach Freeway runs through Wilmington (90744) and West Long Beach (90810), CalEnviroScreen 4.0 top-95th-percentile pollution burden communities. The paper cannot recommend a $2–5B investment in I-710 capacity expansion without addressing this. Required addition in Section 7 (Port Connector Standard) or Section 4 (Case Studies): one substantive paragraph citing SCAG's 2024 RTP EJ analysis, CalSTA's Freight Corridor Environmental Justice Study (2023), and the existing community benefit agreement framework from ICTF/I-710 EIR. The paragraph should acknowledge the equity tension explicitly and recommend that the proposed 23 USC 103(c) port connector classification include a community benefit agreement requirement as a condition of NHPP funding eligibility.

## P2 Items (should address before submission)

**P2.1 — Laredo delay cost decomposition**
Disaggregate the $10.8B figure into: (a) direct delay cost, computable from first principles (truck-hours × operating cost, anchored to CBP data with year specified); (b) JIT disruption cost (indirect, econometric, requires citation to a validated model such as Hummels 2001 or Carballo et al. 2016). Presenting both components separately will both strengthen and make auditable the total figure.

**P2.2 — Laredo jurisdictional analysis**
The I-35 Laredo connector runs through GSA (federal bridge), CBP (POE facility), City of Laredo (approach road), and TxDOT (I-35 junction) jurisdictions. Add a paragraph in the Laredo case study identifying these agencies and the coordination mechanism required. The closest precedent is the FAST Act's Freight Border Infrastructure program (23 USC 117(c)), which authorizes FHWA to fund improvements at land border crossings; the paper should cite this enabling authority.

**P2.3 — Legislative path for 23 USC 103(c) amendment**
Add one paragraph acknowledging that the proposed NHS category amendment requires primary legislation. Identify the surface transportation reauthorization (IIJA successor, anticipated 2026–2027) as the natural vehicle. Note that the Senate EPW Committee and House T&I Committee have joint jurisdiction and that the amendment would need to be included in the reauthorization markup.

**P2.4 — Construction cost model disaggregation**
Break the $2–5B/connector range into components: land acquisition (by urban density tier), structure cost (per lane-mile), utility relocation, and environmental mitigation. A simple two-column table (low-cost case: greenfield/low land cost; high-cost case: dense urban/high land acquisition) would make the range analytically useful rather than merely indicative.

**P2.5 — Climate exposure and 2050 stress-test**
Add a half-page section addressing: (a) the FHWA 2021 Climate Adaptation Guidance requirement that major capital investments consider 50-year and 100-year future climate conditions; (b) the specific climate exposures of the highest-priority connectors (I-710: minimal direct flood exposure but Port SLR exposure; Houston Ship Channel: post-Harvey flood exposure documented; Savannah: Savannah River floodplain proximity); (c) a brief statement that the proposed port connector standard should incorporate FHWA climate adaptation requirements as a design-basis criterion.

**P2.6 — Operational alternatives for I-710**
Before recommending physical capacity expansion at I-710, add one paragraph evaluating whether the existing POLA/POLB truck appointment system (DTSC Turn Time Program) has measurably reduced peak V/C, and whether enhancement of appointment-system incentives could defer the need for physical widening. This does not require new research — cite the existing POLA/POLB appointment system evaluation literature.
