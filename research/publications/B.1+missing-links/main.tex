\begin{abstract}
Twenty percent of Americans --- 66.5 million people --- live in counties whose population centroid is more than 30 miles from any interstate highway on-ramp. We present the first systematic gap analysis of the US interstate network at the county level, using Census Bureau county centroids, TIGER/Line highway geometry, and the ROUTE 12-dimensional corridor scoring framework. We identify four geographic gap zones (Northern Tier, Appalachians, Rural Gulf South, Rural West), classify gap counties into four types, and rank 12 priority proposed corridors by coverage efficiency (gap population served per billion dollars of estimated investment). A Northern Tier interstate aligned with the US-2 corridor would serve 324,862 North Dakotans and 502,001 Montanans currently in gap counties; I-69 completion would bring 3.5 million Texans within 30 miles of a direct Gulf-to-Midwest corridor. Gap counties have mean C3 (Economic Opportunity) scores 40\% above non-gap counties, confirming that the coverage gap is an economic opportunity gap. Twelve priority corridors would bring 85\% of gap-county population within 30 miles, at a combined estimated cost of \$152 billion.
\end{abstract}
