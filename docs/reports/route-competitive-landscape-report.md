# ROUTE Competitive Landscape Report

## Thesis

ROUTE should not position itself as another traffic data product, GIS platform,
travel demand model, engineering design suite, or smart-mobility operations
dashboard.

Those markets already have credible incumbents. ROUTE's clearer wedge is the
missing layer between planning data and executive action: a service-network
decision package that converts priority places, route roles, promise targets,
resilience failures, investment packages, and evidence boundaries into a
reviewable client readout.

## Competitive Frame

| Lane | Representative Products / Firms | What They Sell | ROUTE Posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobility data and analytics | INRIX, StreetLight, Replica | Observed or modeled movement, origin-destination flows, travel time, congestion, safety, and mobility dashboards. | Use as inputs or partners; do not compete as a raw mobility-data vendor. |
| GIS, LRS, and asset systems | Esri ArcGIS Roads and Highways and agency GIS stacks | Authoritative spatial systems, linear referencing, roadway assets, dynamic segmentation, and map management. | Integrate with or cite; do not compete as the system of record. |
| Travel demand and network modeling | PTV Visum, TransCAD, regional travel models | Demand modeling, network assignment, multimodal planning, scenario analysis, and technical model outputs. | Complement with executive service-role translation and evidence-bound promise design. |
| Engineering and design software | Bentley OpenRoads and civil design suites | Survey, drainage, utilities, roadway design, construction-driven engineering, and detailed project design. | Stay upstream; ROUTE is not engineering design or construction readiness. |
| Smart mobility operations | Iteris ClearMobility and ITS platforms | Monitor, visualize, optimize, and manage mobility infrastructure operations. | Complement with strategic service hierarchy and investment-package framing. |
| Consulting and grant advisory | Engineering firms, planning consultants, grant writers | Studies, plans, design packages, funding applications, and delivery support. | Partner or package upstream evidence logic; avoid replacing statutory authority or engineering scope. |

## What ROUTE Adds

ROUTE's product value is not a better map by itself. It is a disciplined
translation workflow:

- buyer priorities become a tiered service hierarchy,
- road and node roles become planning promises,
- promises become evidence-labeled holds, candidates, and next proof tasks,
- failure modes become resilience obligations,
- packages become staged investment options,
- dashboards become specifications for what leadership should track,
- unsupported claims are blocked before they become sales language.

This is why the recommended first product remains a 90-day service-network
diagnostic rather than a generic dashboard subscription.

## Positioning By Buyer

| Buyer | Incumbent They Already Know | ROUTE Opening |
|---|---|---|
| Toll road / turnpike operator | Traffic analytics, toll operations, asset systems, incident management. | "What service promise should this corridor be managed against, and which failures deserve investment first?" |
| Port authority / terminal district | Truck flow studies, terminal access studies, GIS, freight plans. | "Which land-side access failures break the port promise, and what evidence is missing before making an operating claim?" |
| Managed-lane or concession operator | Demand forecasts, toll analytics, operations dashboards, concession reporting. | "Which reliability promise is being sold, what risks degrade it, and what should be in the next service package?" |
| State DOT planning office | Travel models, STIP/TIP workflows, asset systems, safety plans, corridor studies. | "How do top cities, gateways, rural regions, and freight nodes fit into a coherent T1/T2/T3/T4 service network?" |
| Freight corridor coalition | Commodity studies, corridor plans, grant narratives, port and warehouse data. | "How do multiple jurisdictions agree on service roles, failure modes, and proof asks without pretending the plan is final?" |
| Infrastructure sponsor / investor | Technical due diligence, demand studies, engineering reports, legal agreements. | "Which service promises and evidence gaps define the opportunity before valuation, ROI, or project-readiness claims are promoted?" |

## Differentiation

| Question | Incumbent Answer | ROUTE Answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is happening on the network? | Mobility data, traffic speeds, O/D flows, congestion, incidents. | Use those as evidence inputs, then ask what promise and tier the network should support. |
| Where is the road asset? | GIS, LRS, asset inventory, authoritative agency records. | Respect the system of record, then add service-role and evidence-bound claim layers. |
| What might demand look like? | Travel demand models, assignment, scenario tools. | Translate model outputs into planning promises, holds, and decision packages. |
| Can this be designed and built? | Engineering design, environmental, right-of-way, construction documents. | Hold construction readiness until downstream engineering and statutory gates close. |
| What should leadership fund first? | Plans, studies, grant narratives, project lists. | Rank investment packages by service role, failure mode, evidence posture, and claim risk. |

## Partnership Strategy

ROUTE should treat most incumbents as input suppliers, downstream partners, or
systems of record.

| Partner Type | Good Relationship | Bad Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Mobility-data vendor | ROUTE ingests or cites travel-time, O/D, safety, freight, or congestion evidence. | ROUTE claims to replace the data vendor. |
| GIS / LRS platform | ROUTE uses authoritative network identity and asset references. | ROUTE claims to be the authoritative agency map. |
| Travel-model owner | ROUTE translates model scenarios into service promises and evidence holds. | ROUTE claims to replace calibrated demand models. |
| Engineering consultant | ROUTE creates the upstream service-network package that informs later studies. | ROUTE claims design, NEPA, right-of-way, or construction readiness. |
| Grant / finance advisor | ROUTE structures evidence and package logic before a funding ask. | ROUTE claims eligibility, award likelihood, or ROI before evidence exists. |

## Sales Implication

The pitch should avoid "we are a better planning platform." That invites a
feature-by-feature fight with entrenched products.

The stronger pitch is:

> ROUTE gives infrastructure leaders a service-network decision package before
> they spend years arguing over disconnected maps, studies, dashboards, and
> project lists.

The buyer does not have to replace existing systems to use ROUTE. They bring
their existing maps, traffic data, asset systems, priorities, and studies;
ROUTE organizes them into a reviewable promise hierarchy with visible evidence
boundaries.

## Competitive Red Lines

Do not claim that ROUTE:

- replaces INRIX, StreetLight, Replica, Esri, PTV, TransCAD, Bentley, Iteris, or
  agency systems,
- has paying customers,
- has market share,
- is preferred by agencies or private operators,
- is procurement-ready,
- is an official planning authority,
- proves legal SLAs,
- proves positive ROI,
- proves traffic demand,
- proves construction readiness,
- proves funding eligibility,
- has external validation.

## Boundary

This report is a competitive-positioning hypothesis. It uses public product
descriptions as category references, not as endorsements, procurement analysis,
market sizing, customer validation, or proof that ROUTE can displace any
incumbent product.
