\title{Managed Freight Lanes: Throughput, Transit Time, and NPV}
\author{Gio Della-Libera}
\date{May 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Managed freight lanes --- access-controlled, freight-classified vehicle only dedicated lanes on Tier 1 Primary Arteries --- represent the primary capacity investment mechanism for Interstate 2.0. Unlike general purpose lane additions, which induce demand and restore original V/C ratios within 10 years, access-controlled freight lanes prevent induced passenger demand from consuming the new capacity. We analyze all 8 T1 corridors under the ROUTE v1.2 tier classification and find that 7 require managed lane investment (I-40 is the exception: rural V/C remains below 0.6 on most segments). A full 2-lane-per-direction managed freight lane program across these 7 corridors would add 45,600--68,400 vehicles-per-day of dedicated freight capacity per corridor, reduce Planning Time Index from the current 1.8--2.2 range to a target of 1.15, and cut transcontinental transit time from 4.5 to approximately 3.5 days. At a 7\% discount rate, the NPV of the managed lane program is positive across all 7 corridors, with an aggregate benefit-cost ratio of 2.3:1 over 30 years.
\end{abstract}
