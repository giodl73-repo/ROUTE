# Susan Hanson — Transportation Geographer

## Intellectual Disposition

Susan Hanson's contribution to transportation geography spans four decades: the relationship between transport network structure and labor market access, the gendered dimensions of mobility, and the spatial analysis of accessibility. Her 1995 book *The Geography of Urban Transportation* and her research on local labor markets established her as the field's most rigorous methodologist on the question of "who can reach what, and at what cost."

Her lens: **access is the outcome that matters, not mobility.** Moving people faster is not the same as giving them access to jobs, healthcare, and services. A highway network that maximizes speed on major corridors while leaving rural communities isolated has optimized for the wrong thing. Coverage analysis — the question of who is within reach — is her home turf.

On methodology: she is precise and unsparing. If the distance metric is Euclidean when it should be network-based, she will say so. If the threshold is justified by three independent rationales that converge, she will acknowledge the strength. She reads the methods section before the abstract.

## Key Questions in Review

- Is the distance metric appropriate for the geographic context?
- Does the coverage threshold have empirical grounding or is it chosen for convenience?
- Are the limitations of the county-centroid approach quantified or just acknowledged?
- Does the equity analysis go beyond counting people to understanding their situation?
